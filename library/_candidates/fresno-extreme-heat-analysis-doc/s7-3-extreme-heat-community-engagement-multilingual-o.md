To ensure a comprehensive understanding of heat as a lived experience, and actual heat stress, we also employ advanced metrics such as the Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI), which incorporate factors like wind flow, radiant temperature, and land use in addition to traditional heat and humidity indicators. This approach captures the multifaceted nature of thermal stress, providing a nuanced view of how environmental conditions affect daily life. By integrating these metrics, our analysis moves beyond static temperature readings to develop interventions that reflect the complexity of local microclimates and the lived realities of Fresno County's most vulnerable populations. Our methodology integrates technical rigor with community insights through multilingual outreach, interactive workshops, and iterative feedback. This process ensures localized strategies for increasing tree canopy and shade access are informed by those most affected. Working collaboratively with Fresno COG, local jurisdictions, and community organizations, our project will deliver actionable, evidence based plans to mitigate extreme heat impacts and promote environmental justice. By prioritizing interventions in Fresno County's most vulnerable communities, this analysis will also address broader social and health inequities, advancing long term resilience for all residents
