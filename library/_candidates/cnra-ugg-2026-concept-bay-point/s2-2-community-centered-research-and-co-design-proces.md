Before design begins, the project anthropologist will develop and carry out a focused humanistic research plan combining structured observation with brief, voluntary conversations with transit riders. Conducted at different times of day and under varying heat and shade conditions, the research will document where people wait, how they seek shade, use seating, move through the site, maintain sightlines, and manage accessibility needs or personal belongings. Conversations with willing riders will help explain these observed patterns rather than relying on assumptions about behavior. Findings will be summarized through an annotated site-use map and concise design recommendations. They will be brought into the project's community co-design process, where riders and neighboring residents can interpret, challenge, and validate the findings and help determine how they should influence trees, planting, shade, seating, circulation, accessibility, and visibility. A limited post-installation study will assess changes in site use, comfort, and shade-seeking behavior and inform recommendations for other Bay Point transit stops
