This multi-benefit project will also have a workforce development component, with cohort participants comprised of local unhoused community members, and will be incorporated into project design and implementation. Participants, led by our partner SOS Richmond, will receive paid, hands-on experience helping establish and steward project improvements. Rather than creating a stand-alone training program, the project will use real construction, planting, and maintenance activities as opportunities for workforce development. The project will also explore the use of environmental monitoring equipment at selected transit stop locations to better understand localized conditions along the corridor. Sensors may be used to track air temperature, surface temperature, and air quality before and after project implementation. This information will help evaluate the effectiveness of shade trees, green infrastructure, and other project improvements while providing valuable data to support future planning and climate adaptation efforts. Where feasible, workforce participants may assist with monitoring activities and data collection as part of their hands-on project experience
