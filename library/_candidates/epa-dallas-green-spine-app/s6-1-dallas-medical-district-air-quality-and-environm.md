The area is a non-attainment area for air quality and ranks in the top 10%-20% nationally for particulate matter (PM); ozone, nitrogen dioxide; diesel particulate matter; traffic proximity and volume; Superfund, RMP facility and hazardous waste proximity; and underground storage tanks.5 • The 2022 American Lung Association "State of the Air" report found that the Dallas metro area's rankings were worse for some of the most harmful and widespread types of air pollution: particle pollution and ozone.6 The Dallas Fort-Worth Metro area is 13th out of 228 metropolitan areas for high ozone days. (US EPA, 2020). • The right of-way currently has only 7% tree canopy cover compared to the minimum 30% needed to effectively reduce ambient air temperature and/or mitigate air quality (Texas Trees Foundation, 2017). • Excessive stormwater runoff occurs on the roadway during periods of extreme rainfall and the current design and expanse of the roadway contributes to the issues of nonpoint source runoff pollution for the Knights Branch tributary which flows through Arlington Park on its way to the Trinity River that lies 1 mile to the west. It is well documented that poverty and environmental factors contribute heavily to the vulnerability and negative health outcomes for the disadvantaged communities that surround the medical district. The 2022 Dallas Community Health Needs Assessment shows that the project area covered by zip code 75235 regularly ranks at the top for HIV, mental health, hypertension, diabetes and asthma. The zip code area which includes Arlington Park, Love Field also ranked the highest in Medicaid and Uninsured Emergency Department discharges (over 10,000) and the second highest number of unhoused patients (1,506) in 2021. Parkland Hospital's Asthma Registry projects that between 2019 and 2024, the volume of outpatient services will increase by 11% by 2024 and 25% by 2029.
