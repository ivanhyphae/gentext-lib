COMMUNITY DRIVEN INVESTMENTS FOR CHANGE 1.1 Community Overview Community Description The Southwestern Medical District in Dallas, Texas is a vitally important regional healthcare, biomedical research and education hub located 3.3 miles northwest of downtown Dallas. Three major hospital systems anchor the area, which employ over 37,000 people, enroll over 3,600 students and attract over 3.3 million patient visits each year. (Southwest Medical District, 2021) The district and its surrounding neighborhoods within a two-mile radius are home to over 43,000 people. The neighboring population consists of 55% people of color and 36% low income, compared to 39% and 33% for the US, respectively. Community Challenges Harry Hines Boulevard, built in the 1940s, was one of the first "highways" built in Texas and the first divided highway in Dallas. Originally designed as a rural freeway, over the past three decades the surrounding land use has changed to a higher-density urban environment. The grade- separated cloverleaf design of the intersection with Inwood Road – which cuts the medical district into quadrants – was designed solely for motor vehicles prohibiting the use of alternate forms of travel like walking and cycling. Furthermore, the interchange has constrained the development of a district-wide mobility system for non-motor vehicles with the SWMD plagued with sizable gaps in sidewalks, few formal bike lanes/paths, and no buffers between pedestrians and fast-moving vehicle traffic.
