Ambrose Center Park demonstration project will deploy site-scale heat vulnerability modeling and nature-based cooling infrastructure to address extreme heat in Bay Point's primary community hub. Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI) simulations and digital twin modeling will be conducted before and after construction to quantify measurable reductions in surface temperature and thermal stress across the park's most heavily used areas, including the community center plaza, parking lot, ballfields, and community garden. New cooling infrastructure will include a shaded community pavilion adjacent to the concession stand, permeable decomposed granite gathering areas with integrated native plantings, shaded seating nodes along pedestrian pathways, and a shaded outdoor classroom connected to the existing Ambrose Community Garden. Impervious surfaces will be replaced with permeable, heat-reducing materials and native groundcovers to further lower surface temperatures. California native, drought-tolerant, climate-ready tree species will be selected to ensure long-term canopy resilience under projected future climate conditions
