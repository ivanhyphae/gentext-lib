NSF SBIR Phase I and II Grants: AdaptOS Development (2020–Present)\ \ Hyphae's AdaptOS platform, developed with funding from NSF SBIR Phase I and II grants, enables community-based organizations (CBOs) and municipal partners to address environmental risks such as extreme heat, air pollution, and flooding with precision and scalability. This platform bridges gaps in traditional environmental planning by combining hyperlocal data collection, modeling, and design to ensure interventions are both cost-effective and impactful. > The AdaptOS framework is iterative, cycling through measurement, simulation, implementation, and assessment. Modeling is at the core of this process, serving two essential functions. First, it optimizes sensor placement by predicting pollutant dispersion patterns, ensuring accurate monitoring and data collection. Second, it allows communities to simulate the effects of proposed interventions,such as urban greening, truck rerouting, and shade structures,before implementation, providing clarity on which solutions will yield the greatest impact and benefit-to-cost ratio. > > This approach addresses a persistent challenge faced by many environmental initiatives, as exemplified by Hyphae's work on the West Oakland Community Air Action Plan (WOCAP). While WOCAP mandated significant regulatory concessions and outlined 89 pollution mitigation strategies, decision-makers lacked the empirical tools to determine which interventions would have the most significant impact at the hyperlocal level. AdaptOS fills this gap, demonstrating how interventions like tree canopy, vegetation, and traffic patterns can be configured to protect environmental health effectively. > > By combining rigorous modeling with actionable design strategies, AdaptOS transforms environmental data into solutions tailored to community needs, ensuring measurable public health and environmental benefits. Hyphae has successfully deployed this platform in projects across Oakland, Stockton, and Dallas, delivering scalable frameworks that can guide future environmental resilience efforts.
