Beyond heat relief, the Ambrose Memorial Park Urban Greening Project will deliver a range of additional community and environmental benefits. A primary tributary of Willow Creek, intersects the park, and proposed green stormwater infrastructure and native riparian plantings will reduce flood risk and stormwater runoff during major rain events while improving flood management for the upper Willow Creek watershed and restoring critical native vegetation and habitat. The project is further strengthened by a recent land donation from Contra Costa County, which expands the project footprint. Vegetated barriers/Tree buffer will reduce PM 2.5 and air pollution exposure from highway 4 not only for park users but also for the daily commuters and travelers using the adjacent Bay Point/Pittsburg BART station and the Delta de Anza Regional Trail. This multi-benefit project therefore extends its positive impacts well beyond the park boundary, improving air quality, flood resilience, habitat, and green connectivity for a broad cross-section of the Bay Point community and the wider regional trail and transit network. In partnership with the Contra Costa Resource Conservation District (CCRCD), the Ecostewards program will lead implementation of the green infrastructure improvements, creating local jobs and workforce training opportunities for Bay Point residents while also providing environmental education programming for the community. The Ambrose Recreation and Park District will partner in the long-term maintenance of these improvements, ensuring the project's benefits are sustained for generations. Together, these elements represent a truly multi-benefit project that advances climate resilience, ecological restoration, community health, and economic opportunity in one of Contra Costa County's most vulnerable communities
