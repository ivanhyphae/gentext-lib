Ambrose Memorial Park is one of the primary recreational facility for the Bay Point community, owned and maintained by the Ambrose Recreation and Park District. It features a playground, picnic tables, BBQs, bathroom, large grass areas, and 'Lion's Den' picnic area with picnic tables, large BBQ and electricity. It is also home of the Ambrose Aquatic Center. Bay Point is a disadvantaged community under SB 535, and the park's location adjacent to Highway 4 contributes to significant urban heat island conditions. The proposed urban greening project will implement nature-based solutions including expanded tree canopy, drought-tolerant native plantings, and enhanced shaded green spaces to provide critical cooling refuge, improved air quality, and equitable access to healthy outdoor recreation for one of Contra Costa County's most heat-vulnerable communities. The need for this project is strongly supported by multiple data sources documenting Bay Point's extreme heat vulnerability: The 2015 Contra Costa Health Services report Climate Change Vulnerability in Contra Costa County: A Focus on Heat identifies Bay Point as having high overall vulnerability to extreme heat, with elevated indicators for poverty, children under age 5, linguistic isolation, asthma, and treeless areas (pg 32) Climate projections using Scripps Institute of Oceanography/Cal-Adapt data forecast 21–35 days over 100°F annually by the 2090s for the Bay Point area (pg 13) The Climate Vulnerability Map designates the project site as having HIGH future vulnerability to extreme heat, with 15.6% of days projected to exceed 100°F by 2050, elevated PM 2.5 exposure, and urban heat island classification The California Heat Assessment Tool rates the project area HIGH for Heat Health Events with a score of 3.55 (exceeding the 75th percentile) and MODERATE for the Heat Health Action Index with a score of 45.41 Ambrose Memorial Park serves as one of the primary recreational green spaces for Bay Point, a disadvantaged community that faces some of the most severe and compounding heat vulnerabilities in Contra Costa County. The park's location adjacent to Highway 4 contributes to urban heat island conditions, elevated PM 2.5 exposure, and limited tree canopy, conditions that disproportionately burden a community already identified as having high indicators for poverty, linguistic isolation, asthma, and large populations of children under age 5. Without intervention, these conditions will worsen: climate projections forecast 21–35 days over 100°F annually in Bay Point by the 2090s, and the California Heat Assessment Tool rates the project area HIGH for Heat Health Events. The Ambrose Memorial Park Urban Greening Project will provide meaningful and direct benefits by creating tangible, community-identified cooling infrastructure, expanded tree canopy, drought-tolerant native plantings, and shaded gathering spaces, that directly reduce heat exposure for park users, improve air quality, and expand access to safe outdoor recreation for vulnerable residents including young children, low-income families, and linguistically isolated households. These benefits directly respond to community needs, build long-term climate resilience, and would not occur without grant funding, as the Ambrose Recreation and Park District serves a low-income community with limited resources to implement large-scale greening improvements independently. Beyond heat relief, the Ambrose Memorial Park Urban Greening Project will deliver a range of additional community and environmental benefits. A primary tributary of Willow Creek, intersects the park, and proposed green stormwater infrastructure and native riparian plantings will reduce flood risk and stormwater runoff during major rain events while improving flood management for the upper Willow Creek watershed and restoring critical native vegetation and habitat. The project is further strengthened by a recent land donation from Contra Costa County, which expands the project footprint. Vegetated barriers/Tree buffer will reduce PM 2.5 and air pollution exposure from highway 4 not only for park users but also for the daily commuters and travelers using the adjacent Bay Point/Pittsburg BART station and the Delta de Anza Regional Trail. This multi-benefit project therefore extends its positive impacts well beyond the park boundary, improving air quality, flood resilience, habitat, and green connectivity for a broad cross-section of the Bay Point community and the wider regional trail and transit network. In partnership with the Contra Costa Resource Conservation District (CCRCD), the Ecostewards program will lead implementation of the green infrastructure improvements, creating local jobs and workforce training opportunities for Bay Point residents while also providing environmental education programming for the community. The Ambrose Recreation and Park District will partner in the long-term maintenance of these improvements, ensuring the project's benefits are sustained for generations. Together, these elements represent a truly multi-benefit project that advances climate resilience, ecological restoration, community health, and economic opportunity in one of Contra Costa County's most vulnerable communities. Cool Bay Point: Ambrose Memorial Park Heat Mitigation and Urban Greening Project Project Description Ambrose Memorial Park is the primary recreational facility for the Bay Point community, owned and maintained by the Ambrose Recreation and Park District (ARPD). The surrounding neighborhood (Census Tract GEOID 06013313203, 06013314105) in Bay Point that utilize the park is a severely disadvantaged and disadvantage community, and the park's location adjacent to Highway 4 contributes to significant urban heat island conditions, elevated PM 2.5 exposure, and limited tree canopy. The Cool Bay Point: Ambrose Memorial Park Heat Mitigation and Urban Greening Project will implement a comprehensive, data-driven, and community-informed approach to deliver nature-based cooling solutions for one of Contra Costa County's most heat-vulnerable communities. The project will proceed through an integrated work plan encompassing advanced heat vulnerability modeling, environmental monitoring, community engagement, site design, planting, and long-term stewardship. Modeling Advanced multi-scale heat vulnerability modeling will form the analytical foundation of the project, providing unprecedented precision in identifying heat-vulnerable areas and guiding the design of targeted cooling interventions at Ambrose Memorial Park. The modeling approach will integrate three levels of analysis: low-resolution countywide thermal comfort analysis using ERA5 and NWS HeatRisk data to establish baseline heat exposure patterns across the Bay Point region; medium-resolution satellite-derived surface temperature mapping using ECOSTRESS and Landsat imagery to identify neighborhood and block-scale hotspots within and surrounding the park; and hyper-local 1-meter resolution Urban Thermal Comfort Index (UTCI) modeling using 3D digital twin simulations of the park, the adjacent Highway 4 corridor, and connecting pedestrian routes to the Bay Point/Pittsburg BART station and Delta de Anza Regional Trail. These simulations will incorporate wind flow, humidity, radiant temperature, and shade modeling to produce a three-dimensional understanding of heat stress experienced by park users, active transportation users, and BART commuters. Social vulnerability data , including poverty rates, age distribution, linguistic isolation, and asthma hospitalization rates documented for Bay Point in the 2015 Contra Costa Health Services heat vulnerability report , will be integrated with environmental modeling to produce a comprehensive heat vulnerability assessment that reflects both physical and social dimensions of extreme heat risk. Pre- and post-planting simulations will also be run to quantify the anticipated cooling benefits of proposed greening interventions, directly informing planting design and providing a predictive evidence base for how the project will reduce harm from extreme heat for park users and the surrounding community. Monitoring A robust monitoring program will be established to track and evaluate the project's effectiveness in reducing heat exposure and improving environmental conditions at Ambrose Memorial Park over time. Baseline conditions will be documented prior to construction, including surface temperatures, tree canopy coverage, air quality, and stormwater runoff along the Willow Creek tributary that intersects the park. Post-planting monitoring will measure changes in thermal comfort, shade coverage, and air quality buffering from Highway 4 to quantify the project's impact on reducing extreme heat exposure for park users. Monitoring data will be integrated with the project's digital twin modeling platform to enable ongoing evaluation of cooling benefits and long-term climate resilience, and will be shared with ARPD, CCRCD, and community stakeholders to support adaptive management and inform future greening investments in the Bay Point community. Community Engagement Community engagement will be a central and ongoing component of the project, ensuring that the design, implementation, and stewardship of greening improvements directly reflect the needs and priorities of Bay Point residents. ARPD, in partnership with CCRCD and local community-based organizations, will establish a project working group and execute a comprehensive public outreach plan. Engagement activities will include pop-up listening sessions, community events, and surveys conducted in English and Spanish to ensure accessibility for Bay Point's linguistically diverse population, including the high proportion of Spanish-speaking households with limited English proficiency identified in the 2015 Contra Costa Health Services vulnerability report. Community mapping will identify priority gathering spots, pedestrian routes, and areas of greatest heat exposure within and adjacent to the park, directly informing the placement of shade trees, green spaces, and cooling infrastructure. Feedback gathered during community engagement will be integrated into the project design and used to ensure that proposed interventions reflect community-identified needs, including safe outdoor recreation spaces for young children, cooling refuge for elderly residents, and accessible green space for low-income families who lack air conditioning at home. Trusted community institutions including local schools, churches, and clinics will serve as outreach partners, consistent with engagement strategies identified as effective in the Monument Corridor case study documented in the 2015 Contra Costa Health Services report. Design The project design will translate heat vulnerability modeling results and community input into a comprehensive site plan for Ambrose Memorial Park that maximizes cooling benefits, ecological function, and community usability. The design will integrate expanded tree canopy, drought-tolerant native plantings, shaded gathering spaces, and green stormwater infrastructure to address the overlapping challenges of extreme heat, urban heat island effect, Highway 4 air pollution, and flooding along the Willow Creek tributary. Planting locations will be optimized using 1-meter UTCI simulations and digital twin modeling to strategically position trees and vegetation where they will provide maximum shade, thermal comfort, and air quality buffering for park users, pedestrians, and cyclists traveling to and from the adjacent BART station and regional trail. The design will also incorporate recently donated land from Contra Costa County adjacent to the park, creating a tree buffer along Highway 4 that will reduce PM 2.5 exposure and provide additional cooling benefits for park users, BART commuters, and Delta de Anza Regional Trail users. Green stormwater infrastructure along the Willow Creek tributary will restore riparian habitat, enhance native vegetation, reduce flood risk during major rain events, and improve flood management for the upper Willow Creek watershed. Species selection will prioritize California native, drought-tolerant, climate-ready trees and plants suited to the site's conditions and projected future climate, with no single species exceeding 10% of the overall plant palette in accordance with Urban Greening Grant Program guidelines. Planting and Stewardship Implementation of the project's greening improvements will be led by ARPD, in partnership with the Bay Point Garden Club, the Caribbean South America Hispanic Counsel, and the Contra Costa Resources Conservation District's Ecostewards Program, providing a powerful model for community-based stewardship that extends the project's benefits well beyond physical infrastructure. The Ecostewards program along with Garden Club and the S. America Hispanic Counsel will deploy trained crews to install trees, native plantings, and green stormwater infrastructure at Ambrose Memorial Park, creating local jobs and workforce training opportunities for Bay Point residents while simultaneously delivering environmental education programming for the community. Tree planting will be sequenced and supported through up to three years of plant establishment activities, including irrigation, mulching, and maintenance, to ensure high survival rates and the early establishment of meaningful shade canopy. By directly reducing surface temperatures, expanding tree canopy, and creating shaded gathering spaces in a park that serves as the primary outdoor recreation resource for a community with limited access to air conditioning, the project will provide critical cooling refuge for Bay Point's most vulnerable residents , including young children, elderly residents, low-income families, and linguistically isolated households , during extreme heat events that are projected to intensify significantly in the coming decades. ARPD will oversee long-term stewardship and maintenance of all improvements in partnership with the CBOs, ensuring that the cooling, ecological, air quality, and community health benefits of the project are sustained for generations
