One idea that continues to emerge is the distinction between physical infrastructure and relational infrastructure. *While this grant primarily funds physical improvements, successful implementation ultimately depends on the relationships, stewardship, governance, community participation, cultural intelligence, and long-term care that surround those investments.* This presents a distinct opportunity for I-ReLab to strengthen the cultural, relational, and implementation frameworks necessary for climate infrastructure to succeed over the long term. Furthermore, we can serve as a collaborative design partner, *utilizing insights from relational infrastructure data to guide development.* As I continue reviewing the program, I will further explore how concepts such as Belonging, Harm Reduction, stewardship ( "stewardship" should be teased out further because it's actually a flattened term that has nearly lost its power due to overusage), Indigenous land relationships, and community participation intersect with physical infrastructure to create more durable and community-supported climate resilience initiatives.
