To address these inequities, our Fresno County Extreme Heat Analysis will combine advanced heat vulnerability assessment with robust community engagement. Using high resolution data from ERA5 satellite, ECOSTRESS satellite imagery, Google StreetView, aerial surveys, LiDAR and other geospatial datasets, we will analyze both urban and rural heat island effects, tree canopy coverage, and socioeconomic indicators to identify vulnerable hotspots. These areas of interest will then be taken back to the Working Group and wider community to select the five Project Areas designated for detailed planning and analysis as specified by the RFP. To ensure a comprehensive understanding of heat as a lived experience, and actual heat stress, we also employ advanced metrics such as the Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI), which incorporate factors like wind flow, radiant temperature, and land use in addition to traditional heat and humidity indicators. This approach captures the multifaceted nature of thermal stress, providing a nuanced view of how environmental conditions affect daily life. By integrating these metrics, our analysis moves beyond static temperature readings to develop interventions that reflect the complexity of local microclimates and the lived realities of Fresno County's most vulnerable populations.
