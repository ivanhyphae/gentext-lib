***Changes to Grant Structure***
  - EHCRP now uses “Early Transformational” (planning) and “Advanced Transformational” (implementation) labels instead of “planning” and “implementation” tracks.  
  - Planning grants now must include a pilot project or demonstration of tangible outcomes.  
  - Implementation grants must include a planning component with demonstrated vision for broader systems or policy change.  
  - All projects—regardless of tier—are considered “transformational” and must reduce heat illness or death, with an emphasis on policy change, ongoing programming, and long-term systemic impact.  
***Measurement and Metrics***
  - CalAdapt / CalHeat scoring no longer required.  
  - Projects must show harm reduction qualitatively (stories, descriptions) 
      - \[and quantitatively (data, studies)?\] 
          - unclear…  
  - Funders welcome non-traditional metrics, but applicants must still explain how reductions in heat illness/death will be measured.
***Infrastructure Definitions***
  - Confusion around what counts as “infrastructure”
  - Nature-Based Solutions (NBS) are explicitly considered infrastructure.
  - Infrastructure can be green, gray, or hybrid.  
  - Funders were vague about definitions; clarification will be requested during the call.  
  - Brent raised the question of whether sensor equipment, mobile infrastructure (e.g., farmworker oases, mobile shade), or even trellis/hedgerow systems might qualify.  
***Funding Structure***
  - Planning grants: 
      - $500–600K total; pilot project limited to 25% of budget (i.e., \~$125–150K for hard costs).  
  - Unclear whether planning grants allow any hard costs beyond the pilot.  
  - Implementation grants have different restrictions but still require soft/hard cost ratios.  
  - Overhead policy and eligible expense categories (e.g., outreach, data modeling, personnel) need confirmation.  
***Partnerships***
  - Every application must include a CBO–Government partnership.  
      - If lead applicant is a CBO, must partner with city/state/regional entity.  
      - If lead applicant is a public agency, must partner with a CBO.  
  - Outreach can be funded only if tied to infrastructure delivery.  
  - Current partners under consideration:
      - Existing Arvin-Lamont-Shafter work could be leveraged either as implementation or planning.

# Concepts

We’re developing a proposal for a small EHCRP planning grant focused on extreme heat and pavement reduction. The project would start with site-specific modeling—stacking data on heat, flooding, and canopy gaps to identify high-need locations—and end with a built demonstration. Depending on site conditions, we can replace the pavement with a range of green infrastructure solutions: expanding tree canopy, planting swales and buffers, converting excess blacktop in schools to green space, or adding trees and permeable surfaces along sidewalks, medians, and alleys.  
The project would combine sensor installation and heat modeling with community-led site planning, culminating in a demonstration project tailored to local conditions. Depending on site and need, this could include one or more adaptation strategies such as depaving, shade infrastructure, or nature-based design. We're looking to partner with organizations who can help shape and implement this effort.
We’re looking for partners to help pilot a small-scale depaving and adaptation project that starts with site-specific modeling—stacking data on heat, flooding, and canopy gaps to identify high-need locations—and ends with a built demonstration. Depending on site conditions, we can deploy a range of nature-based solutions: removing pavement to expand tree canopy, planting swales and buffers in underused roadways, converting excess parking into green space, or adding trees and permeable surfaces along sidewalks, medians, and alleys. Each project is tailored to local context, designed for impact, and meant to be replicable.
