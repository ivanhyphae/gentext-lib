# **1. Introduction and Purpose**
This Initial Study of Environmental Impacts is being prepared to support environmental review of the Ambrose Center Park Green Infrastructure Enhancement Project pursuant to the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA), the CEQA Guidelines (14 Cal. Code Regs. § 15000 et seq.), and applicable policies of Contra Costa County. Consistent with CEQA guidelines, this Initial Study describes the site improvements and potential environmental impacts of the project. 
Given the voluntary, proactively, environmentally driven goals of the project, we intend to seek the negative declaration or mitigated negative declaration CEQA pathway.
Ambrose Recreation and Park District (ARPD) is the Project Sponsor. The Lead Agency, Contra Costa Resource Conservation District (District) responsible for CEQA compliance is identified in Section 2, below.

## **2.4 Project Location**
The proposed project is located at Ambrose Center Park, 3105 Willow Pass Road, in the unincorporated community of Bay Point, Contra Costa County, California. The park site is generally bounded by Alves Lane and adjoining residential neighborhoods to the west and east, Willow Pass Road to the north, and the Delta de Anza Regional Trail corridor to south. An offsite parcel owned by Contra Costa County and East Bay Municipal Utility District (EBMUD)?, located adjacent to the southern boundary of the park, is also included in the project area to accommodate a coordinated drainage improvement described in Section 5.9. The project site and surrounding area, including site boundaries, adjacent roadways, and the offsite parcel, are shown on the vicinity map and site plan, appended to this Project Description.
## **2.5 Surrounding Land Uses**
Ambrose Center Park is surrounded primarily by residential neighborhoods to the south, west and east as well as commercial uses, Willow Pass Road to the north, and the Delta de Anza Regional Trail, a regional bicycle and pedestrian corridor, along the southern site margin. The existing site is generally flat to gently sloping and includes existing ballfields, playgrounds, a parking lot, a building/community facility, and informal drainage feature which contribute to intermittent flooding.
The park site and offsite parcel carries a Parks and Recreation designation under the Contra Costa County General Plan Land Use Designation, consistent with its existing and continued use as a public park. The neighborhood to the west is designated as Residential-Medium High Density, the neighborhood to the east is designated as Residential-Medium Density, and the adjacent businesses to the west are designated as Mixed Use Community Specific. The parcel to the north across Willow Pass Road is designated as Light Industrial.
# **3. Project Background**
Ambrose Center Park is an existing, developed neighborhood park serving the Bay Point community, a disadvantaged community under SB 535. The park lies within the historic watershed of a minor tributary to Willow Creek Central draining to the Suisun Bay marshlands, an existing drainage ditch along the site's western margin is undersized and itself floods, and the Project site was ranked among the highest priorities in Bay Point in the Contra Costa County Stormwater Resource Plan's preliminary prioritization analysis (CCRCD 2023).
Portions of the park's drainage, circulation, and recreational facilities have not kept pace with community needs, accessibility requirements, and stormwater management best practices. The park's western and eastern margins convey surface water from the site and from adjacent off-site areas, and intermittent flooding has affected the park and community center. In addition, existing bicycle and pedestrian connections between the park, surrounding neighborhoods, Willow Pass Road and the regional Delta de Anza Trail are incomplete or substandard.
The District proposes a coordinated set of site improvements to address these conditions. The proposed project would upgrade drainage and hydrologic function, improve accessibility and circulation for pedestrians, bicyclists, and vehicles, enhance recreational facilities, and formalize a bicycle/pedestrian connection to the Delta de Anza Trail. The project would also coordinate with Contra Costa County and EBMUD and the adjacent neighborhood to the west to address flooding on the adjoining parcels through modest grading and drainage improvements.
# **4. Project Objectives**
The following objectives describe the underlying purpose of the project:
  - Improve on-site stormwater conveyance and water quality at Ambrose Center Park through new swales, bioretention basins, and coordinated grading, while respecting the operational needs of the existing ballfield and other recreational facilities.
  - Reduce intermittent flooding affecting the park's western margin and the adjacent Contra Costa County/EBMUD parcel by coordinating grading and drainage improvements across the shared boundary.
  - Improve accessibility throughout the park, including ADA-compliant access to the playground area, entry points, and circulation routes.
  - Establish a safe, formalized bicycle and pedestrian connection between the park, Willow Pass Road, Alves Lane, and the Delta de Anza Regional Trail, consistent with Bay Trail design standards.
  - Upgrade the park's northern entry and parking lot to improve vehicular, bicycle, and pedestrian access, traffic control, and fire/emergency access.
  - Enhance the quality and cohesion of gathering areas near the building, playgrounds, and storage building through landscaping, plaza, and circulation improvements.
# **5. Project Description**
The project consists of a coordinated set of grading, stormwater, circulation, hardscape, and planting improvements across nine areas of the park and one adjoining offsite parcel, summarized in Table 1 and described in detail in Sections 5.2 through 5.10. Sitewide grading/stormwater, circulation/hardscape, and planting improvements that apply across multiple areas are then described in detail in Sections 5.11 through 5.13. 
## **5.1 Project Overview**
The project is a voluntary, grant-funded stormwater and site improvement project; the stormwater improvements described in this section are not required by regulation or permit condition and are being pursued at the District's initiative and successful grant funding from the CA Department Water Resources Integrated Regional Water Management (IRWM) program to improve stormwater quality and quantity management. The project is planning to provide on-site retention of 60-80,000 cubic feet (CF) of water.
The project would upgrade Ambrose Center Park and its parking lot in Bay Point, Contra Costa County, California, to improve stormwater performance, safety, accessibility, compliant fire access, and community use for the Bay Point community. The project integrates green infrastructure with improved multimodal circulation, planting, and site amenities.

## **5.2 **
## **Area A – Entry**
The northern entry to the park would be redesigned to provide dedicated bicycle and ADA-accessible access points, a gated vehicle entry, and a small gathering/arrival area with seating, bicycle parking, and permeable pavers. Entry upgrades also include bicycle parking and wayfinding signage. These improvements are intended to formalize and improve the primary point of arrival to the park while controlling vehicle access. Additional entrance and traffic-calming improvements at Willow Pass Road are described in Section 5.12.
## **5.3 Area B – Water/Path at West Site Margin**
The western margin of the site carries the primary surface flow conveying water from the entire park as well as from specific off-site areas. Grading in this area would be coordinated with neighboring parcels to improve both hydrologic function. Water would be facilitated using weirs, footbridges, and natural materials such as stone and logs, and portions of the existing fencing would be replaced. This area is part of the interconnected retention basin, bioswale, and bioretention system described in Section 5.11.
## **5.4 Area C – Building Vicinity**
The area surrounding the existing park building would receive targeted landscaping upgrades, including the addition of trellises and trees planted in new tree wells, to improve shading and the vicinity's overall character. Trench drains would also be added near the concession stand to alleviate known flooding issues, as described in Section 5.11.
## **5.5 Area D – Parking Lot**
The parking lot would be reconfigured to accommodate 88 spaces using 45° and 60° angled parking with 14- to 24-foot drive aisles, establishing designated one-way and two-way circulation segments. The lot would include bioretention islands for stormwater treatment, dedicated landscaped pedestrian connections, permeable paving in select stalls, five ADA-accessible spaces (including a van-accessible space) near building entries, and traffic control measures including compliant fire access. A shared bicycle route connecting Willow Pass Road would also be established through this area, along with new pavement striping and directional decals to improve circulation. New gates would allow temporary closure of portions of the lot for farmers markets and community events while preserving emergency and maintenance access. Additional detail on the parking lot's circulation design and stormwater storage capacity is provided in Sections 5.11 and 5.12.
## **5.6 Area E – Playgrounds and Shed**
Circulation and gathering space near the shed and playgrounds would be improved to create a cohesive area that accommodates pedestrian, bicycle, and surface water flow through plaza-style design cues. Playground access would be modified with a new accessible ramp, and small bridges would be added to allow crossing of drainage channels. Maintenance and emergency vehicle traffic would be accommodated through gated access points.
## **5.7 Area F – Baseball Field and Adjacent Areas**
Grading on and around the baseball field would be modified to improve surface water flow while respecting rules and best practices applicable to baseball facilities. Bioretention planting would be installed at low points near the field, located outside the area of play, to avoid interference with field operations. 
**5.8 Area G – Bike Path to Delta de Anza Trail**
An improved, 586 foot, 8-foot-wide bicycle route, shared with pedestrian flow, would route through and around proposed bioretention areas and connect through the western corner of the site to the existing Delta de Anza Trail. The route would be designed consistent with Bay Trail standards and would feature decomposed granite shoulders. 
**5.9 Area H – Offsite Swale (EBMUD Parcel)**
The project includes coordination with the East Bay Municipal Utility District (EBMUD) to improve existing intermittent flooding on the parcel adjacent to the park, through coordinated grading. This offsite work is included in the project description because it is directly related to, and a reasonably foreseeable component of, the on-site drainage improvements described in Sections 5.3, 5.7, and 5.11; the on-site and off-site drainage systems function as integral parts of the same overall stormwater management strategy.
## **5.10 Area J – Western Site Spur**
The bicycle route established in Area G would continue and interface with Alves Lane, with gates provided to control motor vehicle access at this connection point.
# **6. Detailed Description by Scope Work**
## **6.1 Proposed Grading and Stormwater Improvements**
**Start with the existing problems we are trying to solve from your SW report. Onsite/Offsite flooding, low point in the watershed.**
The existing 60-inch storm drain pipe would remain in place and would be supplemented with parallel drainage to accommodate extreme storm events. Drainage would be primarily diverted and retained away from existing pipe system; however, site grading would also need to tie into that pipe, and portions of the existing drainage infrastructure would be improved and/or replaced as part of the project. 
Regrading would establish an interconnected system of retention basins, bioretention cells, and bioswales with low-flow channels and tiered terraces designed to slow, convey, and infiltrate runoff. Bioswale side slopes would be maintained at 3:1 where feasible, and constrained areas would be designed to protect tree driplines, utilities, and property boundaries. Regrading, crowns and cross-slopes in the parking lot would direct water away from walkways and toward treatment areas. Bioretention areas would maintain a consistent 1 percent longitudinal grade, and check dams within the bioswales. Trench drains would be added at key locations,  including near the concession stand and other stormwater treatment areas, to alleviate known flooding issues. New pipes and inlets would connect the proposed bioswales, bioretention areas, and retention basins to the existing storm drain inlets.
As discussed in Section 5.1, the proposed grading currently provides between 60-80,000 cubic feet of storage.  Total volume will be finalized in next stages of design and engineering as final grades, slopes, details are worked out. The site will be graded between 3-8ft below existing grade in some locations. 
## **6.2 Proposed Circulation Improvements**
As described in Section 5.5, the parking lot would be reconfigured to optimize parking traffic flow and safety using 45° and 60° angled parking with 14- to 24-foot drive aisles, establishing designated one-way and two-way circulation segments. A two-lane entrance from Willow Pass Road would incorporate lane dividers for traffic calming, and proposed raised crosswalks would be used to reduce vehicle speeds. Fire truck access routes would maintain the minimum required width of 20 feet and required turning radii per Contra Costa County standards.
Five ADA-accessible parking spaces, including a van-accessible space, would be provided near building entries, and permeable paving would be used in select parking stalls. Planting strips would separate the curb and sidewalk, and pedestrian paths would provide access for the public to interact with the stormwater features. An 8-foot-wide bicycle path would route through and around the bioretention areas, connecting to the existing Delta de Anza Trail (Section 5.8). Signage, steps, boulders and nature paths would invite public engagement and educational opportunities with the site's green infrastructure. New gates in the proposed parking lot would allow temporary closure for farmers markets and community events while preserving emergency and maintenance access (Section 5.5).
## **6.3 Proposed Site Furnishing Improvements**
Extensive site furnishing improvements are proposed. Some of these will hopefully be covered by the current grant we have, some of them are being included in the design and planning but may need to be covered by a future grant. These improvements would include bicycle parking, seating, informational and wayfinding signage. Water fountains, trellis fort shade and bathroom improvements  (Section 5.2). Existing chain-link fencing will be repaired, removed or upgraded. 
## **6.4 Proposed Planting  & Irrigation Improvements**
Stormwater features would be planted with a water-tolerant, native and adapted planet palette of trees, grasses and shrubs.. Bulb-outs and islands in the parking lot would include layered planting, and planting strips with new trees would line walking paths. New planting and vegetated trellises would be provided at the concession stand and southern entrance of the community center. Mature trees would be preserved and protected, and new tree plantings would provide additional shade in pedestrian areas along the building edges, parking lot, and proposed stormwater features.
The irrigation system would be upgraded, with new plantings supported by the sitewide well water supply, new controllers, and pumps remaining near the front building and baseball field. 
# **7. Construction and Operational Characteristics**
## **6.1 Construction Characteristics**
Construction would involve grading, installation of bioretention and swale features, placement of moderate fill at the northern margin of Alves Park and the baseball field, paving and re-striping of the parking lot and bicycle routes, installation of decomposed granite surfacing, fencing replacement, and construction of small pedestrian bridges, weirs, gates, and site furnishings. 
Best management practices (BMPs) for stormwater control during construction, along with design features will minimize construction-related impacts (e.g., dust control, tree protection, and erosion control).
## **6.2 Operational Characteristics**
Following construction, the park would continue to operate as an existing public recreational facility, with the addition of improved stormwater treatment features, upgraded circulation, and a new regional trail connection. Operational and maintenance responsibilities for the new bioretention and swale features, gates, and the offsite EBMUD drainage improvement should be clarified between the ARPD, Contra Costa County, and EBMUD, including a description of the maintenance agreement or easement expected to govern the shared drainage features at Area H.
