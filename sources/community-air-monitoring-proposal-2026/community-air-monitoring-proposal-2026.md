## ✨ Summary
The meeting reviewed a plan to combine Charles’s narrative and Brent’s deployable rapid-response system into a single proposal for community-driven air quality monitoring and modeling. Participants discussed expanding monitoring from existing sites into neighboring communities including Richmond, Benicia, Martinez, and Pittsburgh and emphasized a technical approach built on broad PM2.5 low-cost sensor deployment complemented by targeted research-grade instruments and mobile sampling. Charles recommended adding measurements for reactive gaseous contaminants from renewable diesel refineries, notably formaldehyde and other aldehydes, and suggested using Miro laser spectrometers for mobile and indoor ozone‑forming VOC characterization.
The group examined grant and partnership requirements and considered separate but conceptually linked proposals across jurisdictions, with coalition letters of support prioritized in scoring. Funding scale and scope were discussed for opportunity-category grants in the $500K–$5M range, with emphasis on differentiating this effort from past monitoring by combining sophisticated optical and mobile instruments, chronic emissions tracking, and acute incident response while minimizing administrative burden on partner organizations. Attendees also compared parallel proposals for household interventions and biomonitoring, evaluated the feasibility and cost of adding soil and blood lead testing, and confirmed outreach and partner-engagement communications and a recurring meeting cadence to sustain proposal development.
