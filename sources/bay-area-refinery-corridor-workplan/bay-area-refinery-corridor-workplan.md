Joint Workplan Narrative: Bay Area Refinery Corridor Community Air & Wind Study

Richmond–Carquinez Strait–Delta Corridor (Richmond → Martinez → Pittsburg → Antioch → Crockett)

1\. Overview and Purpose

This joint workplan proposes a three-year, community-driven air quality and wind study across the Bay Area refinery corridor, spanning Richmond through Martinez and the Carquinez Strait to Pittsburg and Antioch, including major industrial sources such as refineries and power plants. The project is structured as a coordinated Opportunity Grant effort under both the Richmond Chevron Local Community Benefits Fund and the Benicia-area fund, and is explicitly designed to extend and amplify the ongoing CARB Cycle 5 Community Air Grant (G23-CAGP-0537) led by the Rodeo Citizens Association (RCA).

The project responds to a clear environmental justice reality: low-income, industrially burdened communities along this corridor experience disproportionate exposure to air pollution, including elevated rates of asthma, cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and adverse birth outcomes. At the same time, these same communities suffer from a systematic lack of air monitoring infrastructure, particularly compared to wealthier areas such as Walnut Creek, Mill Valley, and the Berkeley Hills.

The purpose of this joint workplan is to:

Reduce exposure disparities by building a dense, community-centered PM2.5 monitoring network

Understand pollutant transport using advanced wind modeling and computational fluid dynamics (CFD)

Empower communities with real-time data and participatory science

Inform regulatory action by CARB and BAAQMD for future monitoring, enforcement, and planning

2\. Alignment with CARB Cycle 5 and LCBF Goals

This proposal is a direct extension of the CARB Cycle 5 workplan, which established a citizen-science air monitoring coalition and technical modeling framework for the Carquinez Strait region.

The LCBF Opportunity Grants will expand this work geographically and technically by:

Extending the monitoring network westward to Richmond and eastward to Pittsburg and Antioch

Increasing sensor density and filling spatial gaps in disadvantaged communities

Scaling up the wind and CFD modeling framework led by Hyphae Design Laboratory

Integrating additional community engagement, health mitigation, and indoor air quality components

The project fulfills core LCBF goals by:

Reducing air pollution exposure through improved monitoring and targeted interventions

Improving public health outcomes via data-driven mitigation strategies

Building economic and community resilience through workforce development and citizen science

Advancing integrated, community-defined solutions across multiple jurisdictions

3\. Geographic Scope and Environmental Justice Context

The study area includes:

Richmond (Chevron refinery area)

Hercules, Rodeo, Crockett (Carquinez Strait corridor)

Martinez and Benicia (refinery cluster)

Pittsburg and Antioch (power plant corridor)

This region contains:

One of the highest concentrations of industrial sources in Northern California

Multiple refineries (currently five, potentially four pending closure)

Power plants in Crockett, Pittsburg, and Antioch

Major transportation corridors and marine shipping routes

Despite this concentration of emissions sources, the corridor exhibits:

Significant monitoring gaps in PM2.5 and co-pollutants

Limited access to indoor air filtration resources

High cumulative pollution burdens in CalEnviroScreen-designated disadvantaged communities

4\. Technical Approach: Integrated Monitoring and Modeling

4.1 Community-Based PM2.5 Monitoring Network

The project will deploy an expanded network of:

\~200 PurpleAir PM2.5 sensors

Co-located calibration with regulatory monitors (BAAQMD / Bay Air Center)

Supplemental sensors for future phases (VOC, NOx, ultrafine particles)

This builds on the CARB Cycle 5 baseline network and addresses known limitations of sparse regulatory monitoring systems by increasing neighborhood-scale spatial resolution .

Key outputs:

Real-time public dashboards

Hotspot identification

Longitudinal seasonal datasets

4.2 Wind Monitoring and CFD Modeling

Hyphae Design Laboratory will lead a multi-scale wind and pollutant dispersion modeling effort, including:

Complex terrain modeling (10–25 km scale) for regional transport

Microscale urban CFD (1–3 km scale) for neighborhood exposure

Integration of:

GIS and LiDAR-based digital twins

Meteorological datasets

Emissions inventories (refineries, traffic, power plants)

The modeling framework uses OpenFOAM CFD simulations to analyze airflow, turbulence, and pollutant transport pathways .

Key innovations:

Linking community sensor data to CFD validation

Mapping plume pathways from sources to receptors

Identifying high-exposure zones for intervention

4.3 Iterative Monitoring–Modeling Feedback Loop

A central innovation of this project is its adaptive, iterative design, in which:

Community input identifies hotspots

Sensors are deployed

Data informs CFD models

Models refine sensor placement

Results are shared and re-evaluated

This continuous refinement improves both data accuracy and community relevance .

5\. Community Engagement and Governance

The project is fundamentally community-driven, not researcher-driven.

5.1 Community Science Research Council (CSRC)

A regional council will include representatives from:

Richmond, Rodeo, Crockett, Martinez, Benicia

Pittsburg and Antioch

Community-based organizations and residents

The council will:

Guide sensor placement and modeling priorities

Interpret findings

Develop a community-informed regulatory agenda

5.2 Public Engagement Framework

Over three years, the project will conduct:

12–16 community meetings and workshops

Bilingual (English/Spanish) outreach

Participatory mapping of pollution hotspots

Training in sensor use and data interpretation

This approach ensures that lived experience is treated as data, not anecdote.

6\. Public Health and Mitigation Strategies

Beyond monitoring, the project will implement direct health interventions, including:

Distribution of indoor air filtration units

Guidance on exposure reduction strategies

Identification of high-risk populations (schools, elderly, medically vulnerable)

The data generated will support:

Future interventions targeting NOx, VOCs, ozone, and toxics

Integration into regional health planning

8\. Policy and Regulatory Impact

This project is designed to directly inform:

BAAQMD monitoring expansion and siting decisions

CARB AB 617 community designation and planning

Future Community Air Monitoring Plans (CAMP)

Local Community Emissions Reduction Plans (CERP)

By combining community-generated data with advanced modeling, the project creates a scientifically credible foundation for regulatory action.

9\. Long-Term Vision

This Opportunity Grant initiative establishes a scalable, corridor-wide air quality intelligence system that:

Closes environmental monitoring gaps

Builds durable community capacity

Creates a replicable model for other refinery and industrial corridors

It transforms historically underserved communities from passive recipients of pollution into active producers of actionable environmental data, aligned with both CARB and LCBF mandates.

Conclusion

The Richmond and Benicia Opportunity Grants, when integrated with the CARB Cycle 5 framework, create a regionally unified, community-led air monitoring and modeling system across the Bay Area refinery corridor. This workplan directly addresses longstanding environmental justice inequities by pairing technical rigor with community leadership, ensuring that the communities most impacted by air pollution are also those most empowered to understand, monitor, and ultimately reduce it.

2\. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (1–2 PAGES)

Executive Summary

The Bay Area refinery corridor—stretching from Richmond through the Carquinez Strait to Pittsburg and Antioch—is one of Northern California’s most industrialized and environmentally burdened regions. Communities along this corridor face disproportionate exposure to air pollution from refineries, power plants, and transportation sources, resulting in elevated rates of asthma, cardiovascular disease, and other chronic health conditions.

Despite these risks, these same communities suffer from a critical lack of air monitoring infrastructure, leaving pollution exposures under-documented and inadequately addressed.

This proposal establishes a three-year, community-driven air monitoring and wind modeling initiative that directly addresses these inequities. Building on the California Air Resources Board (CARB) Cycle 5 Community Air Grant, the project expands monitoring and modeling efforts across the full refinery corridor using a combination of:

Dense PM2.5 sensor networks

Advanced computational fluid dynamics (CFD) modeling

Community-led research and governance structures

The project is led by the Rodeo Citizens Association (RCA) in partnership with CAMN, CCRCD, and Hyphae Design Laboratory.

Key Outcomes

Deployment of a high-resolution community air monitoring network

Identification of pollution hotspots and exposure pathways

Development of actionable data for regulatory agencies (BAAQMD, CARB)

Direct public health interventions (e.g., indoor air filtration)

Empowerment of residents as citizen scientists

Innovation

This project integrates community knowledge with advanced scientific modeling, creating a feedback loop in which residents help define monitoring priorities and interpret results. By combining real-time sensor data with CFD modeling, the project provides an unprecedented level of detail in understanding how pollution moves through complex terrain and impacts specific neighborhoods.

Impact

The initiative will:

Reduce exposure disparities

Improve health outcomes

Build long-term community capacity

Inform regional air quality policy

This project represents a scalable, equity-centered model for addressing air pollution in industrial corridors across California.
