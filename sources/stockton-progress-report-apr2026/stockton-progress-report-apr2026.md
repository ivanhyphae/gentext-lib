# Stockton Urban Greening & Vegetated Buffer Projects
**Progress Report: Project Start (August 2025) – April 2026**
Prepared for: Valley Air District
Prepared by: Hyphae, in partnership with Little Manila Rising (LMR)
# Overview
This report summarizes work performed on the two grant-funded projects — the Urban Greening Project and the Vegetated Buffer Project — from project kickoff through the end of April 2026. Both projects are funded by the Valley Air District and are being implemented in partnership with Little Manila Rising (LMR) in Stockton, CA. Because the two projects share a delivery partner and a common geography, some coordination and data-gathering activities support both projects; these are called out separately below.
*This summary is based on a review of internal meeting notes from August 2025 through April 2026. It reflects work in progress as well as completed milestones — items are labeled accordingly. Please verify details before submission to Valley Air District, as some notes reference tentative next steps rather than confirmed deliverables.*
# Urban Greening Project
## Data Collection & Aggregation
  - Stood up a shared GIS/mapping platform (Felt) as the working base map for both Hyphae and LMR.
  - Aggregated and layered in: property/parcel boundary data, a roads layer (built in-house due to incomplete Caltrans road data), Caltrans truck and traffic volume counts at major freeway intersections, CO2 dispersion data (used as a proxy for particulate pollution), and Ecostress satellite heat-map data (surface temperature).
  - Began building an NDVI / Leaf Area Index model to assess vegetation health and canopy density, and started assembling a 3D “digital twin” neighborhood model (heat and shade) using LiDAR data flown over Stockton.
  - Pulled in LMR’s existing tree inventory data (approximately 40–50 trees to date) from LMR’s PlanetGeo database, and began reconciling/aligning Hyphae and LMR tree-mapping datasets.
  - Reached out to the City of Stockton for its public tree inventory/GIS layer (request submitted; not yet received as of April 2026).
## Site Prioritization
  - Defined the prioritization methodology: stacking tree canopy, air quality, extreme-heat, and population data to identify highest-need planting areas.
  - Conducted a site visit to Stockton neighborhoods (January 14, 2026) with LMR’s urban forestry team to tour candidate planting areas and review LMR’s existing planting/stewardship practices.
  - Began drafting an initial prioritization map, targeted for delivery in May 2026 (in progress, not yet finalized as of April).
  - Initiated outreach to additional data partners (Ramboll/Shair, Aclima, UC Merced, UCSF, Dawn Air) to expand available air-quality and monitoring data for prioritization; several data-sharing requests remain outstanding.
## Planting Palette, Plans & Boundary Definition
  - Began scoping a tree palette differentiated for residential vs. Caltrans-adjacent sites (not yet finalized).
  - Discussed and began defining the project’s data-collection/analysis boundary (AB617 neighborhood vs. broader South Stockton), balancing the grant’s required focus area against LMR’s broader service area (not yet finalized).
## Community Engagement & Stewardship
  - LMR presented a project status update, including a rough Q2–Q4 placeholder timeline, to the Community Steering Committee (CSC) at its regular meeting (March/April 2026); Hyphae plans to co-present at future CSC meetings.

These are the areas where the same meeting, the same LMR coordination call, or the same drafting session could plausibly touch both projects' scopes of work.
### **Billing recommendations**
1.  **Default rule: bill by deliverable, not by meeting.** If a single meeting covers both projects (e.g., a City of Stockton call that touches tree siting *and* barrier permitting), split the hours by actual time/topic within that meeting rather than picking one project to absorb it all. A simple log entry like "45 min — Urban Greening (tree list approval) / 15 min — Vegetated Buffer (permit discussion)" is defensible if ever audited.
2.  **Keep deliverables physically separate even when the process is shared.** For example, don't write one combined "stewardship plan" — write two (or clearly delineated sections), so each project's invoice ties to a specific, project-labeled output.
3.  **Use a shared time-tracking log with a mandatory project field.** Every timesheet entry should require: Date, Staff, Project (Urban Greening / Vegetated Buffer), Task, Hours, Deliverable/Notes. No entry should be allowed to span both projects without a split.
4.  **For LMR's shared community engagement work specifically** (since both scopes mention engagement plans and LMR outreach support), it may be worth agreeing up front with LMR on a rule of thumb — e.g., outreach events that discuss both trees and buffers get split 50/50, or split proportional to agenda time — so you're not deciding retroactively.
5.  **Document your allocation methodology once, in writing, to Valley Air District** — a short note in each project file explaining "shared activities between these two co-located projects are billed based on \[time-on-topic / agreed split\]" protects you if either invoice is ever questioned.
