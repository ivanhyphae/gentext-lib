**Project Background **
Richmond is an environmental justice community, where residents are exposed to air pollutants from the state’s largest oil refinery, two major highways, metals processing, and a commercial port among others. Richmond is also an under-treed community. Neighborhoods with the least tree coverage are those with the highest pollution exposure and are also the poorest, communities of color, and burdened with health and economic disparities. This means that Richmond residents are burdened with the impacts of industries and transportation routes that serve non-Richmond residents without the protective presence of an urban forest canopy. 
<comment_start id=kix.xn0i0ejj98c1>Groundwork Richmond (GWR) is a community based organization in Richmond that focuses on addressing key environmental justice issues and breaking the cycle of poverty with effective employment training and youth engagement programs. GWR has a depth of experience in tree planting and maintenance (including the sole maintenance contract for all trees planted in the City), strong relationships with impacted communities, community organizations, and the City of Richmond, and a positive reputation for the work we do. **<comment_end id=kix.xn0i0ejj98c1>**
**Project Summary**
This project expands Groundwork Richmond’s tree planting and environmental justice work to the Richmond Parkway, a primary corridor that runs through the western part of the city, drawing a border between industries and neighborhoods including Atchison Village, Iron Triangle, and North Richmond, some of the most polluted and impacted neighborhoods of the City. 
This project aims to: buffer residential areas from pollution; increase tree canopy in under planted neighborhoods; improve pedestrian and bike paths to facilitate non-vehicular traffic; reduce traffic speeds and create safer planted median buffers to prevent or reduce damage from potential traffic collisions; and engage youth in skill building and job training. Our design interventions focus on this corridor because buffering pollution is most effective as close to the source as possible and neighboring sensitive receptors: residential areas, outdoor transit zones (sidewalks and bike paths), and schools. 
This project will focus on three different conditions along the Parkway and design site specific greening approaches. It therefore aims to be a preliminary showcase that can inform future expansion along the full length of the Parkway or other major roadways within Richmond. The showcases are chosen for their typological diversities. 
**“Treefense” \#1: Industrial Boulevard **
South of Atchison Village to W Macdonald Ave
Buffering residents & pedestrians from the rail and parkway: trees in the narrow median and a hedge barrier between the roadway and the sidewalk. For this site, matching funds will fund greening on the neighborhood side of the far east soundwall to further reduce pollution exposure.
**“Treefense” \#2: Bike Path **
Peres Elementary School and the Iron Triangle (Pennsylvania to Lucas Ave)
Buffering bike path and school from offramps and upwind industry: plant parkway median and both sides of the bike path to improve the bike path and further buffer the neighboring school yard. There is an existing soundwall between the Parkway and bike path. 
*Note this project is not proposed for funding within the grant, but we will look for additional research and implementation funds. Likely sources exist in the AB617 Community Emission and Exposure Reduction Implementation process via CARB/BAAQMD.*
**“Treefense” \#3: Freeway **
North Richmond (North Castro St to Sanford Ave)
Buffering sidewalk & residential neighborhood from freeway offramps and upwind industry: plant the narrow median and 20 foot right of way between the Parkway and residences. 
**Project deliveries: **
This project will involve the planting of greening at the above three sites. This will include community engagement and communications by Groundwork Richmond, design and general project management by sub-contracted Hyphae Design Laboratory, and construction management by Groundwork. Construction will include job training of local residents through Groundwork’s programs.

**1. Explain how the project meets the statutory criteria of 1) acquiring, creating, enhancing, or expanding community parks and green spaces, and/or 2) using natural systems, or systems that mimic natural systems.**
This project enhances existing public green spaces (green streets, sidewalks, and bike paths) that have deteriorated over time and that can be significantly enhanced for multiple benefits. It does so by strategically planting these areas: the Parkway median and various City-owned right of ways directly neighboring the Parkway, buffering a sidewalk, and buffering a bike path. 
This project also uses natural systems to improve air quality: it leverages vegetation’s ability to act as both a physical barrier and a physical filter to capture and divert particulate matter, diesel pollution, and VOCs & other air pollutants that have serious health impacts. Richmond residents are over exposed.
Planting will also mimic native ecosystems: we will draw from California plant communities to inform planting choices at various site conditions, including the coastal bluff, pine-manzanita-chaparral, and coastal redwoods.
