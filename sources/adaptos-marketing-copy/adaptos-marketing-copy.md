With over fifteen years of experience working with environmental justice communities across the country, Hyphae is a national leader in evidence-based design. In partnership with communities and academics, they have received funding from the National Science Foundation and the National Institutes of Health to develop an adaptive management platform named adaptOS. This software-enabled platform synthesizes aerial, satellite, and ground-level data to create high-resolution, hyperlocal 3D maps that model local risks to air, water, and soil. In consultation with communities, the platform can then generate adaptive environmental design interventions to maximize human health and biodiversity, as well as mitigate other threats such as extreme heat and flood risk. With the University of Louisville, Hyphae is presently leading the first and largest clinical trial designed to uncover the effects of urban greening on air quality and health-outcomes at a neighborhood scale.

Hyphae Design Laboratory has over 15 years of experience in urban and community forestry, emphasizing environmental justice and resilience planning in underserved communities. Founded in 2008, Hyphae’s work has consistently focused on reducing pollution exposure, creating resilient urban landscapes, and empowering communities with data and design solutions to enhance local environmental health. Working across California, Hyphae has partnered with organizations such as the West Oakland Environmental Indicators Project (WOEIP), Little Manila Rising (LMR), Climate Resolve, and Groundwork Richmond, providing technical expertise, hyperlocal air quality monitoring, and ecological design to address unique urban challenges.
The overarching goals of Hyphae’s urban and community forestry projects include:
  - Reducing air pollution exposure through vegetation buffers and green infrastructure.
  - Enhancing urban resilience against extreme heat and climate impacts.
  - Engaging community members in data collection, advocacy, and the design process.
  - Securing policy changes to support long-term environmental and public health improvements.
Hyphae’s work with our longest partners in West Oakland’s Prescott Project exemplifies this approach. This project uses vegetation buffers and green walls to reduce particulate pollution near residential areas and schools. Through strategic partnerships with local agencies, including the Bay Area Air Quality Management District (BAAQMD) and the California Air Resources Board (CARB), Hyphae helped shape the Community Air Action Plan (WOCAP), incorporating community-centered design and advocating for 89 pollution reduction strategies, such as truck rerouting and increased urban tree canopy.
Similarly, in the Richmond Parkway Urban Greening Project with Groundwork Richmond, Hyphae’s design and planning efforts aim to reduce greenhouse gasses, improve public safety, and increase access to green spaces. The $900,000 CNRA-funded project will plant over 500 trees and engage local youth in urban forestry training, strengthening community stewardship.

Hyphae’s adaptOS is a **climate adaptation platform** that equips **environmental justice communities** with the data and tools they need to lead their own evidence-based urban revitalization efforts. Funded by the National Science Foundation and the National Institutes of Health, the platform combines highly sophisticated measurement and modeling software with design and implementation capacity. The end-to-end project management suite also monitors interventions after completion to guarantee their long-term impact and effectiveness. 
Delivering ecosystem services such as urban greening, stormwater management, and air quality improvement once required costly teams of engineers, epidemiologists, and consultants. By harnessing the latest advancements in modern technology, however, our adaptOS platform extends these vital services to the neighborhoods that most need them, re-localizing power, knowledge, and capital in frontline communities.
Communities face an urgent need for solutions to growing threats like rising temperatures, intensified weather events, and persistent air pollution. These environmental hazards disproportionately affect human health in urban areas, leading to higher rates of respiratory illness, heat-related complications, and other public health crises. Traditional urban planning technology, however, relying on broad census-tract data, lacks the high resolution mapping capacity that allows our software to make site-specific interventions.   
AdaptOS’s urban environmental management technology is revolutionary because it provides precisely this hyper-local, topography-aware data and then delivers targeted interventions at the neighborhood scale. From analysis to design and implementation, AdaptOS guides frontline communities through the entire lifecycle of climate adaptation, transforming toxic environments into healthy, livable cities. 
In neighborhoods across the country, our environmental justice pilot partners are using AdaptOS to combat air pollution, heat island impacts, and a range of other negative health outcomes that disproportionately affect urban communities suffering from the legacy of environmental racism.
AdaptOS operates as a self-correcting **Adapt Cycle**, continuously refining each stage of a project to ensure the best possible outcomes. The cycle includes **Analysis**, where we gather hyper-local, topography-aware data to understand site-specific challenges; **Design**, where we co-create tailored interventions with communities; **Implementation**, where we provide comprehensive support to ensure projects are realized effectively; **Monitoring**, where we track performance using real-time sensor networks; and **Evaluation**, where we assess the long-term impact and make data-driven adjustments as needed. This iterative process ensures that projects remain flexible, adaptive, and aligned with both environmental and community goals.
Analysis
**Overview**:  
The analysis phase of AdaptOS is designed to equip environmental justice communities with the precise data they need to take actionable steps in climate adaptation. It moves beyond standard data collection, delivering targeted insights that are both neighborhood-specific and decision-ready.
**Problem**:  
Communities facing climate hazards often lack access to high-resolution, localized environmental data. While many consultants offer vast amounts of data, they frequently fail to provide the actionable insights necessary for implementing meaningful interventions. This leaves communities overwhelmed by 'analysis paralysis.' They have plenty of data but none of it is actionable or tailored to their specific needs.
**Why Current Approaches Fall Short**:  
Traditional data collection methods often rely on broad census-tract information, which lacks the granularity to address hyper-local environmental challenges. This can result in interventions that are misaligned with specific neighborhood needs, leaving communities without the tailored solutions necessary for climate resilience.
**Our Unique Approach**:  
AdaptOS avoids analysis paralysis by gathering the **right data at the right resolution**. To create a precise picture of each neighborhood, adaptOS combines **LiDAR scanning**, **aerial and satellite data**, **Google Street View**, and **community-generated reports**. Once this data is processed, our **computational fluid dynamics (CFD)** modeling capacity transforms it into actionable insights, enabling communities to understand environmental risks and identify the most effective interventions.
**Community Engagement**:  
AdaptOS ensures that communities are involved throughout the analysis process. Local residents contribute their lived experience, providing valuable context to the data. This participatory approach empowers communities to take ownership of the data and the solutions that emerge from it, ensuring that they are not just passive recipients of information but active participants in the climate adaptation process.

Hyphae’s adapt framework derives from the ecological approach of adaptive management. Adaptive management is an iterative, systematic approach to managing natural resources that aims to improve outcomes over time through a cycle of learning and adjusting. It involves setting clear objectives, implementing management actions, monitoring the outcomes, and using the results to inform and adjust future actions. This approach recognizes the inherent uncertainties in managing ecosystems and seeks to reduce these uncertainties through continuous learning.
As urban ecologists, our  focus is on understanding how urbanization impacts ecosystems, the interactions between human and natural systems in cities, and how ecological principles can be applied to improve urban environments. Urban ecology seeks to enhance the sustainability and resilience of cities by integrating ecological knowledge into urban planning, design, and management.
**Biodiversity:** Examining how urbanization affects species diversity and the distribution of plants and animals in urban areas.
**Ecosystem Services:** Understanding and managing the benefits that natural ecosystems provide to urban residents, such as air and water purification, temperature regulation, and recreation.
**Human-Nature Interactions:** Studying how human activities influence urban ecosystems and how urban nature impacts human health and well-being.
**Green Infrastructure:** Implementing natural and semi-natural systems, like parks, green roofs, and urban forests, to enhance ecosystem services and improve urban living conditions.
**Resilience and Adaptation:** Developing strategies to make urban areas more resilient to environmental challenges, such as climate change, pollution, and extreme weather events.
And in urban areas, the built environment changes conditions block by block.Applying adaptive management to microclimate regulation in urban areas offers numerous benefits. This approach allows for flexible, responsive, and informed decision-making, which can lead to more effective and sustainable urban environments. 
This approach relies on the collection and analysis of data, fostering an evidence-based approach to managing urban microclimates. This leads to more informed decisions that can be fine-tuned over time to enhance their effectiveness.
Adaptive management can help manage urban heat islands (UHIs) by implementing and adjusting strategies such as green roofs, urban forests, and reflective materials. Continuous monitoring and adjustment can optimize these interventions to maximize cooling effects, improving thermal comfort for urban residents.
Urban microclimates can be influenced by vegetation, water bodies, and built infrastructure. Adaptive management can optimize the placement and maintenance of green spaces and water features to enhance air quality by reducing pollutants and increasing oxygen levels.
Effective water management is crucial in urban areas to handle stormwater runoff and reduce flooding. Adaptive management can involve the use of permeable surfaces, rain gardens, and other green infrastructure. By monitoring their effectiveness and making necessary adjustments, cities can improve water infiltration and reduce runoff.
Urban biodiversity can be supported through adaptive management by creating and maintaining habitats suited to various species. Continuous monitoring allows for adjustments to plant and animal habitats, ensuring they thrive even as urban conditions change.
Urban areas are vulnerable to climate change impacts such as increased temperatures and extreme weather events. Adaptive management can help cities become more resilient by allowing for the flexible implementation of climate adaptation strategies, such as increased shading, green corridors, and water retention systems.
Adaptive management can involve local communities in the planning and monitoring processes, fostering a sense of ownership and stewardship. Engaging communities can lead to better-maintained green spaces, increased social cohesion, and improved mental and physical well-being.

# Detailed Summary of "Destroy, Rebuild, Repeat: How to Break the Climate Disaster Cycle"

\#\#\#\# Abstract
Mark Nevitt's paper argues that current disaster and climate laws are outdated and ineffective in addressing climate-induced disasters. He highlights the reactive nature of existing policies, which result in a cycle of destruction and rebuilding, rather than proactive adaptation. The paper proposes a new framework focusing on informing about climate risks, retreating from high-risk areas, and suspending governmental services in such zones.
\#\#\#\# Introduction
Nevitt critiques the U.S.'s current disaster strategy, which he terms "unmanaged retreat," characterized by reactive, disjointed, and ad hoc responses that exacerbate inequalities. He advocates for a proactive and forward-looking approach to break the cycle of "destroy, rebuild, repeat."
