Prescott Greening Planting

Planting & long-term stewardship

Aerial map with green areas highlighted to show the Prescott Greening Planting area. All of the plantings are along Frontage Road. Frontage Road runs alongside the Freeway. Two labels are pointing to water meter locations along the roadway.

Hyphae Design Laboratory has worked in partnership with the West Oakland Environmental Indicators Project (WOEIP) to develop a plan to do a major tree planting along the Frontage Road, which borders the Prescott neighborhood in West Oakland. Planting along this roadway has been desired by the residents of the area for many years.

Planting along this roadway is in alignment with the West Oakland Sustainable Transportation Equity Project (WOSTEP) for several reasons:

Transportation Equity: The WOSTEP grant has the overarching goal of increasing transportation equity for residents in disadvantaged and low-income communities. Planting along the Frontage Road addresses transportation equity in two key ways. The roadway is a connection point between the most densely populated apartment buildings in West Oakland and 7th street, which is the only route to Middle Harbor Shoreline Park, a City of Oakland supported bike destination. Planting along this road will be the first step in making it a more bicycle and pedestrian friendly area.

This planting will also serve as a vegetated buffer between the Prescott neighborhood and the 880 freeway. Blocking the particulate pollution created by vehicular traffic is a key aspect of lessening the disproportionate burdens of air pollution that the residents of the Prescott neighborhood are exposed to. By both encouraging non-vehicular means of transportation and intercepting the pollution caused by vehicular transportation, this project will doubly address transportation related equity .

Greening: The WOSTEP grant has the goal of planting 1,500 trees and shrubs in West Oakland. It has been difficult to reach these numbers through residential and park based planting as a result of the permitting and coordination time that is required for every single site. The Prescott Greening project along frontage road adds 308 trees , which will double the overall number of trees planted.

Stewardship: One of the goals of the STEP grant was to develop a community based stewardship model. The Prescott Greening project will further this goal in multiple ways. Although a part of the City of Oakland’s land, the city has not been able to financially support

maintenance of the area. As a result, neither the irrigation system or the drainage along this area has not been maintained. This has created problems for the survivability of plants in this corridor. In some areas water washes off of Frontage Road, flooding ditches during the heavy rain. These ditches do not drain for weeks at a time, which strains the existing plants. In other areas, the trees have begun to die due to the irrigation system falling into disrepair. Fixing the irrigation and drainage along this corridor will allow for the long-term success of the plantings in this area.

In addition to ensuring the long-term sustainability of the project through irrigation and drainage work, Hyphae used WOSTEP funding to work with community partners to devise long-term stewardship and maintenance strategies for this project.

Prescott Greening Project Description

The Prescott Greening Project will transform the landscape in an existing City of Oakland right-of-way along Frontage road from 7th-14th street. This will include site preparations such as soil remediation, the removal of dead or dying trees, repairing irrigation, doing extensive tree and shrub plantings, and providing a long-term maintenance strategy for the area.

Planting Plan:

The trees along frontage road were assessed by our team as well as hired arborists and tree specialists. The majority of the trees along this road are Redwood trees that are in good condition. In the area between 7th-10th there are some redwood trees that are in poor condition that will be replaced by other trees.

The Hyphae team put together a plant list which was reviewed by the City of Oakland’s trees team as well as arborists and urban ecology advisors. The goal of this plant list was to find trees that thrive well in this drought-prone area, require little water or maintenance after establishment, provide biodiversity, and provide ecosystem services such as food for local pollinators. The final planting plan was reviewed and approved by the City of Oakland’s tree team and Oakland Parks and Recreation Foundation.

Site Preparation:

Site preparations include reducing soil compaction, and the removal and replacement of dead or dying trees and plants. Soil testing has been conducted to determine what, if any, amendments 2

can be added. After planting, the area will be mulched to conserve moisture, suppress weeds, and provide organic matter for long term soil health.

Irrigation:

In order to ensure the best likelihood of planting success, Hyphae worked with city of Oakland’s staff, EBMUD, and Art and Garden Design, a local landscaping contractor, to assess the defunct irrigation systems. The existing meters and backflow preventer were replaced, and a Model Water Efficient Landscape Ordinance (MWELO) compliant irrigation plan was created. The initial piping was installed for the irrigation system, and the rest of the irrigation system will be installed after the final planting.

Project Phases:

After the initial cost estimates for the project, it was determined that only a portion of the Prescott Greening Project could realistically be paid for by the WOSTEP showcase funds. Other showcase projects, such as the planting at the Oak Center Cultural Center, the West Oakland Library, the design and implementation of new tree wells, and installation of monitoring equipment in West Oakland was prioritized. Remaining showcase funds would be used to do a portion of the Prescott Greening. This includes the installation of irrigation, soil preparations, and the purchase of plant materials. The remaining work will be completed using funding from the Metropolitan Transportation Commission (MTC) and the Alameda County Transportation Commission (ACTC), and will be completed in the 2 months after the end of the WOSTEP grant.

Maintenance and Stewardship

Hyphae has particularly prioritized the long-term maintenance and stewardship of projects that were undertaken as part of the WOSTEP grant. The Hyphae team is acutely aware of how the financial burden of maintaining trees has led the city of Oakland to discourage tree planting as a part of new infrastructure projects. In order to build trust with both the community and the city, it is imperative that the trees planted as a part of the STEP grant do not become a safety concern or a financial liability. Hyphae has worked to ensure the long term success of the project in a few key ways: careful planting selection , prioritizing proper site preparation , and strategic partnerships .

Planting Selection:

The only plants that were included in the design were plants that have been proven to thrive in this climate. Hyphae leaned on experts in order to choose the plants that would survive well, while creating the least number of maintenance concerns (such as aggressive roots, disease, or limb failure). The plants that were selected were reviewed by multiple people: Matt Ritter, the author of “A Californian’s Guide to the Trees Among Us”; the city’s Arboricultural Inspector from the Parks & Tree Division, Gordon Matassa; and OPRF’s tree team all reviewed the plant list and gave commentary on the plants.

Proper site preparation:

The ill health of multiple existing trees on the site meant that there was something that was incompatible with those trees and the existing conditions. The Hyphae team worked with Art and 3

Garden Design to implement various preparations on the site that would prevent a similar failure. Soil remediation, fixing an abandoned drainage system, and replacing defunct irrigation will give the new trees and plants the best chance at survival.

Strategic Partnerships- working with the unhoused:

Because paying for any work after the end of the grant was not permitted, Hyphae worked with project partners to lay the groundwork for long term maintenance of the site.

One of the realities of doing greening in the bay area is that green spaces are often the places that unhoused residents will set up camps. The city of Oakland has a very large unhoused population, and that frequently impacts what kinds of improvements to public spaces that the city will allow. There is pressure to exclude benches, hide or remove water sources, and discourage the planting of shrubs where people can hide. This type of hostile design may discourage unhoused people from utilizing a space, but it also discourages everyone else from being able to comfortably use spaces as well. Defaulting to hostile design makes spaces hostile.

The encampments in Oakland often have build up of garbage and waste, create fire risks, and can make local housed residents feel unsafe. But the goal of the environmental justice work that Hyphae does is to improve the health and lives of the people who live in Oakland. The unhoused residents in Oakland are members of this community. Treating these members of the community with respect is not only essential for ethical reasons, but also for creating effective, humane, and sustainable outcomes.

Hyphae connected with Wood Street Commons, an organization formed by formally unhoused residents and allies. The members of this organization visit encampments, do food distributions, clothing distributions, and work to advocate for the unhoused. Hyphae met with the Wood Street Commons team in order to work on putting together a way that the unhoused could be considered and included in the long-term management of the Prescott Greening and other planting projects in Oakland.

Together Hyphae and Wood Street Commons created a 2 year plan for management along Frontage Road. After the planting has been completed, Hyphae will be creating a contract with Wood Street Commons, who will employ unhoused residents to do trash clean up 2 times a month along that area. Simultaneously, Hyphae will work with partners to organize volunteer work days that coincide with these trash clean ups. This will allow members of the unhoused community and volunteers to meet each other, an important piece of bridging the gap between these communities.

This partnership will also allow the maintenance of these projects to use funds to directly benefit the unhoused residents in Oakland. The goal is to pay these residents, rather than pay other people to remove them, to help support more stability for these vulnerable communities in the long-term.

Hopefully this collaboration will allow for unhoused residents to be more involved in creating practical solutions for our shared urban environment in the future.

Prescott Greening in Context

Long-term investment into environmental justice

Hyphae and WOEIP have been working on environmental justice issues in West Oakland for more than a decade, and will continue to work on these issues past the end of the WOSTEP grant. Transforming a community and addressing environmental equity requires long time investment into projects: having an overarching vision that can be achieved through various funding sources.

Hyphae and WOEIP have been working on the larger goal of using air quality modeling, monitoring, and evidence based design to improve human and environmental health. Hyphae has been researching and evaluating emissions and exposure risks, and then using that data to design and model greening interventions. After modeling interventions, Hyphae and WOEIP are working together to fund the implementation of these greening projects.

AB617 and the West Oakland Community Action Plan

Greening along the Frontage Road is a part of a larger project happening in the Prescott neighborhood. The Prescott Greening Project came from the intention of fulfilling strategies outlined in the West Oakland Community Action Plan (WOCAP).

West Oakland experiences disproportionately high levels of air pollution, more than three times higher than the Bay Area average. Areas within the Prescott neighborhood are hotspots of pollution, with levels of particulate matter as high as 150 to 300% more than the ambient

5

measurements inside West Oakland. In 2018, West Oakland was identified as a first-year priority in a state Community Air Protection Program under Assembly Bill 617. This initiates community-led emissions and exposure reduction programs in neighborhoods most impacted by pollution. West Oakland was chosen as a pilot neighborhood for this program. The West Oakland Environmental Indicators Project (WOEIP) and the Bay Area Air District (BAAD) co-led the development of the West Oakland Community Action Plan (WOCAP), which includes 89 identified strategies to reduce source and exposure to pollution in the neighborhood. The plan was formally approved by BAAQMD and the California Air Resources Board (CARB), and adopted by the City of Oakland City Council in 2019.

One of the strategies, \#12, specifically references the Prescott Project. Strategies relevant to this project include:

● \#10 - The City of Oakland creates a comprehensive, area-wide urban canopy and vegetation plan that identifies locations that trees can be added and maintained, such as parks and along Caltrans' right-of-ways and develops a plan to protect existing trees that reduce exposure to air pollution emissions in West Oakland.

● \#12 The Air District and the West Oakland Environmental Indicators Project intend to implement the green infrastructure project currently under development between Interstate I-880 and the Prescott neighborhood in West Oakland by 2021.

● \#16 - The City of Oakland, in partnership with the Steering Committee, CARB and the Air District, studies the exposure reduction benefit of requiring solid or vegetative barriers to be incorporated into site design between buildings and sources of air pollution.

Frontage Road Planting - First phase of larger goal

The Prescott project aims to actualize the community-led visions in the West Oakland Community Air Action Plan. The primary way to reduce air pollution is with source reduction, but given that this is a longer-term process, there are additional strategies to more immediately reduce resident exposure to existing pollution, such as planting vegetated buffers. The planting along Frontage Road is only the first of several possible planting projects adjacent to the Prescott neighborhood that have been identified.

Frontage Road Planting (Current Phase)

It was determined that revitalizing the planting area along frontage road (replacing trees in poor condition and infilling the planting area) could create an effective buffer for the community from traffic emissions.

Frontage road receives high volumes of truck traffic, and runs adjacent to the freeway: a major source of local pollution. Through the use of Computational Fluid Dynamic modeling, Hyphae evaluated the impact of vegetated buffers in reducing resident exposure to existing pollution. The models indicated that a vegetated buffer in this area could effectively lessen the concentration of particulate pollution downwind in the neighborhood.

There are several additional plantings that the Hyphae team is exploring:

Caltrans Planting

The 880 freeway is a major contributor to particulate pollution in the Prescott neighborhood. In preliminary models, planting buffers in the Caltrans right-of-way was shown to be a promising way to intercept air pollution before it reaches the neighborhood. Initial concept designs are underway.

Frontage Road Diet

A road diet refers to the removal and reduction of lanes in order to slow traffic and increase safety along a roadway. The Frontage Road diet is the proposal to reduce the size of Frontage Road lanes in order to use the excess space for a large vegetated buffer. The Hyphae team has used Computational Fluid Dynamic modeling to develop concept designs for this road diet.

A road diet along this area would not only create a buffer for pollution along the roadway, it would also make the Frontage Road much more safe for bicycles and pedestrians. Reducing lane numbers and sizes helps slow traffic. This proposal also places large vegetated buffers in between the traffic lanes and the bicycle/pedestrian corridors.
