Appliance Upgrade Program:

● One of the key lessons learned during this reporting period was the importance of setting realistic expectations with clients regarding installation timelines. Early in the program, providing specific installation dates or narrow timeframes occasionally led to frustration when unexpected delays occurred. These delays could result from a variety of factors, including contractor scheduling, appliance procurement, coordination between multiple trades, or internal program processes.

Another lesson learned was that some contractors preferred to receive work orders in batches of five or more installations before placing appliance orders with their suppliers. While this approach helped contractors improve efficiency and manage procurement, it also extended the timeline before individual installations could begin.

In response, the program worked closely with contractors to better understand their installation processes, ordering practices, and scheduling requirements. This information allowed staff to provide applicants with more realistic expectations by communicating an estimated installation window of one to two months rather than a specific date.

4\. Discuss any challenges experienced while implementing the grant.

● A challenge experienced during this reporting period was the reliance on High Road HVAC contractors for certain appliance installations. While these contractors possessed the necessary qualifications, not all were willing to coordinate with third-party contractors involved in the installation process. This created barriers for appliance measures, such as ECMs, that require collaboration between multiple trades to complete a successful installation.

For the HVAC contractors who initially expressed a willingness to collaborate, maintaining those partnerships proved difficult. In several cases, communication became inconsistent, and some contractors ultimately withdrew from the project before work could begin. These challenges affected project planning and scheduling, making it difficult to move forward with installations within the anticipated timeline.

As the program evaluated these experiences, it became clear that a different contracting approach would better support program implementation. In response, the program has shifted its strategy to prioritize partnerships with local licensed contractors within Stockton and the surrounding communities. In hopes of working with local contractors it is expected to improve communication, strengthen coordination among trades, provide greater scheduling flexibility, and reduce delays in project implementation.

● Some technical issues were encountered with our Google Sheets. Because multiple Google Sheets are linked together and rely on information syncing between them, it can be difficult to identify where an issue is originating. One issue was that the sheet would mark clients as eligible but would not identify which appliances they qualified for. Another issue occurred when signed agreements were uploaded—they would not appear on the vendor sheet, making it difficult to know when a client was ready for the appliance order to be submitted.

During that time, we are generally unable to move forward with the proposed changes until a decision is made. This uncertainty can delay program implementation and slow progress, as we must wait to determine whether the amendment will be approved before taking further action.

● A concern we previously encountered was the limited guidance we received from the State throughout program implementation. For example, when developing the program eligibility criteria, we were provided with very little direction on what standards or requirements we should rely on. As a result, we spent additional time interpreting program expectations and creating the criteria ourselves. This created uncertainty during the development process and slowed implementation as we worked to ensure the criteria aligned with the program's intent.
