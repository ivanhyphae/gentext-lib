**ADAPT OAKLAND **
**Greening Prescott for Air Quality**
Project Proposal 2021
**\[image\]       \[image\]**
Proposal by Urban Biofilter: a non-profit collaboration between West Oakland Environmental Indicators Project & Hyphae Design Laboratory
**Project Overview**
The following pages outline the background and our overall vision for applying tested, research supported, site specific greening strategies to the Prescott neighborhood of West Oakland, one of West Oakland’s most heavily impacted neighborhoods by air pollution. This project is a part of the West Oakland Community Air Action Plan (WOCAP), an interdisciplinary collaboration under AB617 led by the West Oakland Environmental Indicators Project (WOEIP) and the Bay Area Air Quality Management District (BAAQMD) to implement 89 strategies to reduce air pollution and resident exposure to pollution. This project applies the WOCAP greening strategies to the Prescott neighborhood, an identified impact zone within the WOCAP, with the intention to apply learnings and lessons from Prescott to the rest of West Oakland and beyond. The Prescott vision includes several planting zones and project phases that can be implemented as funding and opportunities become available. We are currently trying to attain funding for the first phase and zone, a research integrated vegetated buffer pilot along Frontage Road. 
**BACKGROUND: Why West Oakland?**
  - West Oakland experiences disproportionately high levels of air pollution, more than three times higher than the Bay Area average.1 
  - Pollution has been linked to serious, long-term health impacts including asthma hospitalization and emergency department visit rates almost twice as high as rates for all of Alameda County2; 
  - Life expectancy for West Oakland residents seven years less than residents of the Oakland hills2; 
  - and statistically significant connections between air pollution and higher risk for heart attack, heart surgery and coronary disease in West Oakland’s elderly population3. 
  - The Oakland Equity Indicators Report scored racial equity in childhood asthma emergency department visits as a one out of one hundred, indicating higher rates for African American children, who make up a significant portion of West Oakland residents, than white children4. 
  - Block by block data, collected by a collaborative project5 between Google, Aclima, the Environmental Defense Fund and West Oakland Environmental Indicators Project, shows areas within the neighborhood that are hotspots of pollution, with levels of particulate matter as high as 150 to 300% more than the ambient measurements inside West Oakland.6

**Community-Led Solutions: West Oakland Community Air Action Plan (AB617)**
In 2018, West Oakland was identified as a first-year priority in a state Community Air Protection Program under Assembly Bill 617, facilitated by the California Air Resources Board. This initiates community-led emissions and exposure reduction programs in neighborhoods most impacted by pollution. West Oakland was chosen as a pilot neighborhood for this Community Air Action Plan. WOEIP and BAAQMD co-led the development of the resulting WOCAP that includes 89 identified strategies to reduce source and exposure to pollution in the neighborhood. The plan was formally approved by BAAQMD and the California Air Resources Board (CARB) in 2019 and is currently being implemented with a diverse Steering Committee of residents, businesses, community organizations, and government agencies.
**Designing “Defensible Space”: Reducing Exposure to Pollution**
The primary way to reduce air pollution is with source reduction, such as electrification of shipping and Port processes. Given that this is a longer-term process, there are additional strategies to more immediately reduce resident exposure to existing pollution, including urban greening. The WOCAP includes several strategies on urban greening (strategies 10, 11, 12, and 16) that involve a neighborhood wide plan, research based implementation, and workforce development integrated into planting and maintenance. Viable greening strategies include vegetated buffers and green walls with plants shown to be effective at removing and or redirecting pollution. Green infrastructure can also offer co-benefits, supported by numerous research studies: beautification, place-making, increased liveability, stormwater and flooding mitigation, access to nature, and related health benefits of lower stress, blood pressure, mortality, chronic illness, etc.

**Our Neighborhood Wide Vision**
Our vision is to strategically green the Prescott neighborhood, the highest pollution hotspot within West Oakland, to improve human and environmental health. This builds off of almost a [decade of greening research and planning](http://adaptoakland.org) in West Oakland and will actualize the community-led visions in the West Oakland Community Air Action Plan. This project is a collaboration between community residents, landowners, local businesses, leading researchers on greening and air quality, and government agencies. It will be implemented in phases, using a data and research informed approach, to most effectively design, plant, and maintain greening strategies to maximize impact and achieve co-benefits for the West Oakland community. The success of this project will lay a strong foundation for greening all of West Oakland and other environmental justice communities across Oakland.   
**Implementation Proposal for Impact Zone 1**
The implementation of this vision involves a series of smaller projects that build off of and complement each other. The map below summaries these different projects and the relevant landowners and project partners to implement each of them. 
\[image\]
**Project Components: **
1.  **Modeling and Analysis (preliminary results summer 2020, advanced modelling 2022)**: 
      - We received a grant through the Strategic Growth Council’s Transformative Climate Communities program for the initial community engagement, design and modeling of viable greening strategies for the Prescott neighborhood. This analysis was completed in summer 2020 and provides schematic design recommendations for the implementation phase. 
2.  **Masterplanning & Entitlements  (2022)**: 
      - **Freeway Buffer** along the full length of Frontage Road on the freeway side of Frontage (CalTrans owned right of way land)
      - **Frontage Road Buffer**: Extending the Frontage Road buffer on City owned land the full extent of Frontage
      - **Existing Housing** (Marcus Garvey): Implementing on-site greening and indoor air filtration at Marcus Garvey Commons, an existing EBALDC owned affordable housing site that is heavily impacted by pollution. 
      - **Planned Housing** (Phoenix Ironworks): Working with the design team and developer for the planned housing at the former Phoenix Ironworks site to integrate landscaping, building orientation, and building design to reduce resident exposure to pollution.
      - **7th Street Corridor** (GoPort Project): The Alameda County Transportation Commission is leading a multi-million dollar project to reroute 7th street between Bay Street Circle and Maritime. We aim to work with the project team to incorporate effective greening mitigations into their project, both on and off-site.
      - **7th Street Corridor** (City-owned ROW): This includes vertical plantings on the canyon walls of 7th street near Frontage road to capture and reduce pollution coming into the neighborhood from 7th Street and the Port of Oakland. 
      - **Dog Park**. (Caltrans land) Vegetated buffer to railyard and accessible dog park in large caltrans under freeway parcel. Access at 14th and 18th.
3.  **Construction Drawings & Initial Project  (2022-24)**: 
      - Caltrans Phase 1 Construction drawings
      - Frontage & \&th Street construction drawings
      - Small Scale Planting on Frontage

**Successful and Needed Funding**
This project began with a $150,000 Transformative Climate Communities grant from the California Strategic Growth Council. In November 2021 we received authorization for $300,000 through the Metropolitan Transportation Commission's Priority Conservation Areas with a  2:1 match of $600,000 from ACTC. We are expecting an additional match of $50,000 from Port of Oakland and $100,000 form Calfire. The entire project is currently estimated to be roughly $30 million dollars. More detailed budget expected summer 2022.
