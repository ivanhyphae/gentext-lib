**SCOPE OF WORK**
To reduce emissions, the 2019 West Oakland Community Action Plan (WOCAP) identified the need to improve the design and safety of local streets by encouraging residents to walk, ride bicycles, scooters, or buses instead of driving cars. This includes implementing the broad array of pedestrian improvements identified in the West Oakland Specific Plan and the 2017 Oakland Department of Transportation (OakDOT) *Oakland Walks\! Pedestrian Plan* (WOCAP Strategy \#56). To support pedestrian health and safety, the WOCAP also calls to develop and implement a comprehensive, area-wide urban canopy and vegetation plan (WOCAP Strategy \#10), which will assist in making West Oakland safe and healthy to walk. The WOCAP also identifies the goal of working with local groups to train residents to maintain biofilters (WOCAP Strategy \#11), and to study the exposure reduction benefit of requiring solid or vegetative barriers to be incorporated into site design between buildings and sources of air pollution (WOCAP Strategy \#16). The Pedestrian Improvements and Urban Greening Proposal will enact these four WOCAP strategies to improve health outcomes of West Oaklanders.  
This project will focus on working with the West Oakland community on four key elements: 
1.  **Implement sidewalk repairs and street improvements:** OakDOT, with support from WOEIP, will identify priority locations for improving sidewalk accessibility and building new sidewalks. In addition, OakDOT will upgrade all intersections with ADA-compliant curb ramps along two miles of West Oakland streets per OakDOT’s equity-driven Three-Year Paving Plan. 
**Task 2.1: Sidewalk repairs and improvements**
Using the sidewalk gap inventory in the *Oakland Walks\! Pedestrian Plan Update,* OakDOT and WOEIP will prioritize the many missing sidewalk locations in West Oakland. OakDOT will use CARB funds to close sidewalk gaps in West Oakland by building a minimum 6’-wide sidewalk connecting West Oaklanders to schools, parks, transit, and other community assets as identified and prioritized by the community. New sidewalks will include new curbs, as needed; street trees, as space allows; and ADA-compliant curb ramps.   
1.  **<comment_start id=kix.5ksbmx4hgmg9>Expand urban greening for pedestrian safety and health<comment_end id=kix.5ksbmx4hgmg9>:** The Oakland Parks and Recreation Foundation (OPRF) will bring together multiple community groups, organizations, and local jurisdictions to plant 2,000 trees or shrubs in the Project Area. Plantings will include a combination of street trees, shrubs acting as vegetative buffers or hedgerows, and trees on privately owned properties within 20 feet of the City right of way. OPRF will coordinate the planting of trees and shrubs on City of Oakland property at sites determined by the local community, the City of Oakland, and other relevant planning documents including *The Oakland Urban Forest Master Plan* and *Adapt Oakland*. In addition, OPRF with West Oakland Green Initiative (WOGI) will actively engage, train, and provide opportunities for community members to directly participate in planting activities. This will include partnering with residents and businesses to coordinate tree and shrub plantings on private property, sharing information and resources for planting and maintenance, and partnering with the City and other jurisdictions for stewardship programming opportunities. Urban Biofilter will provide modelling, engineering, and prioritization guidance for community planting projects to maximize air quality improvements.  
**Task 2.2: Expand urban greening for pedestrian safety and health**
Oakland Parks and Rec Foundation (OPRF) will oversee the planting of 2,000 trees or shrubs in the right of way or on private property within 20 feet of the City right of way. This includes: 
  - Contracting with an ISA-certified project manager whose sole duties will be to oversee the community greening program, which includes planting 2,000 trees or shrubs through a combination of street trees and vegetative medians, and private property. This is a part-time contract position (20 hours per week). 
  - Purchase of planting equipment and supplies 
  - Tree and shrub purchase and planting 
  - Hiring community organizations and partners, including West Oakland Green Initiative, to: 
      - Facilitate tree planting on private properties 
      - Develop educational materials 
      - Train the public to report maintenance concerns 
      - Create a space online where the public can discuss the health benefits of these trees and learn about the natural history, ecosystems, and the flora and fauna these trees attract  
  - Working with Urban Biofilter to design strategies and prioritize planting locations to maximize air quality improvement to: 
      - Apply strategies in the West Oakland Reforestation Plan (WORP), Adapt Oakland, and Oakland Tree study 
      - Update air quality data and greening research to the AB617 WOCAP impact zones 
      - Collaborate with the WOCAP Steering Committee, City officials, community groups, and air quality specialists to prioritize greening locations in West Oakland in order to improve environmental health 
      - Collaborate with relevant city departments and utilities where necessary to integrate lessons learned into policy, Engineering Best Management Practices and operation and maintenance manuals 
      - Provide modelling, engineering, and guidance for community tree planting projects in other areas of the STEP grant per the Urban Forestry Master Plan 
  - Grant administration  
1.  **Implement Heavy Commercial and Pedestrian Corridor Showcase Project: **Urban Biofilter will lead the development of a showcase urban greening project street that will demonstrate and develop greening strategies for commercial corridors. These strategies will aim to mitigate and block pollution created by truck and vehicular traffic; increase pedestrian safety by reducing sidewalk pollution and creating a physical barrier between pedestrians and vehicles; and improve pedestrian and bicyclist experience with a visual barrier and greening. These strategies will include vegetative buffers, hedgerows, and canopy trees that can achieve the above goals without impacting necessary sightlines for view and safety. This project would likely occur along the 7th St. median in West Oakland, currently identified for street redesign by OakDOT, but final project location will be determined with community input. 
**Task 2.3 Heavy Commercial and Pedestrian Corridor Showcase Project**
Urban Biofilter will develop, build, and monitor two to three pilot greening interventions in a Showcase Project (currently proposed for 7th Street corridor, exact location to be determined in collaboration with community members and OakDOT).  
A Showcase Project on 7th Street would include the detailed engineering, permitting, and implementation of greening strategies for freight, commercial, and pedestrian traffic corridors. These strategies will aim to mitigate and block pollution created by truck and vehicular traffic on 7th Street; increase pedestrian safety by reducing sidewalk pollution and creating a physical barrier between pedestrians and vehicles; and improve pedestrian experience with a visual barrier and greening. These strategies will include hedgerows that can achieve the above goals without impacting necessary sightlines for view and safety. Urban Biofilter will work closely with OakDOT to adjust our strategies to meet code requirements and, as needed, to adjust code requirements to enable streetscape strategies that can meet the above goals without compromising safety or other concerns. This will include: 
  - Collaboration with community members, the City of Oakland, WOCAP Steering Committee, and other stakeholders to identify and design locations along 7th Street 
  - Project design and permitting 
  - Oversight of planting installation in collaboration with local tree planting groups, job training organizations, and local contractors  
  - Air quality modelling and monitoring of Showcases before, during, and after implementation 
  - Working with City officials on best management practices and planning guidelines for right of ways 
  - Piloting a stewardship program to maintain, monitor, and evaluate implemented green infrastructure. This will include local resident job training and hiring. 
1.  **Create an urban greening stewardship model:*** *OPRF, Urban Biofilter, and job training organizations will develop long-term, community-based maintenance strategies for the West Oakland urban green spaces. 
**Task 2.4 Urban Greening Stewardship Model**
OPRF, Urban Biofilter, job training organizations like Civic Corps, and fabrication organizations will work together to develop a long-term, community-based stewardship program for West Oakland urban green spaces that would include producing protective tree guards, watering, plant maintenance, and air quality and environmental monitoring and evaluation. 
Taken together, the Pedestrian Improvements and Urban Greening project fulfills multiple strategies identified in the STEP Implementation Grant Solicitation, Appendix E, with a particular focus on Active Transportation - New pedestrian walkways and multi-use paths. It also fulfills STEP Supporting Strategies including: 
  - Community Development - “Workforce development activities for the climate and clean transportation sectors”  
  - Outreach and Education - “Capacity building to implement new or enhance existing clean transportation planning, operations, or infrastructure projects” and “Outreach and education for new or existing clean transportation planning, operations, or infrastructure projects.“ 
Project details that still need to be determined include coordination with the recommendations of the City of Oakland Urban Forest Master Plan (currently under development, due in 2022) and workforce development project details.
