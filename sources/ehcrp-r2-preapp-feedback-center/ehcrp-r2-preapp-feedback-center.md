Dear Lori Chalifoux,

Thank you submitting a pre-application to the ICARP Extreme Heat and Community Resilience Program. The LCI team has reviewed your application materials for Ambrose Center Park Heat Mitigation Project and has determined that your project meets some of the preliminary criteria for submittal. The proposed concept meets our eligibility requirements; however, we encourage you to strengthen other aspects of your project in line with the Program Guidelines to make your submission more competitive.

Please note that the feedback below reflects input from more than one reviewer, so you may notice some repetition or variations in voice between sections. Please read all the feedback together to form a full picture.

Areas for improvement include:

• The description partially supports the selected grant type, but at least one required element is missing or unclear, so the fit cannot be fully confirmed. If an Early Infrastructure project, this means either the Planning Document (an Infrastructure Master Plan or Extreme Heat Action Plan meeting the Program Guidelines Section 3.6 minimum elements) or the Infrastructure Demonstration Project that informs it is missing from the project description (see Section 3). For Advanced Infrastructure projects, this usually means the infrastructure solution isn't clearly identified. The full application will need to identify the specific infrastructure and site. Note also that a Future Implementation Playbook is an additional deliverable that needs to be described in the full application. See Section 4 of the Program Guidelines.  Narrative includes reference to an infrastructure demonstration, but not to a Planning Document as required in Early Infrastructure project. As a possible point of clarity, the primary deliverable for the Early Infrastructure grant is the planning document, which the infrastructure demonstration is intended to strengthen and support. The Full Application will be greatly strengthened by the applicant clarifying this understanding within the narrative and including specific plans for the completion of the mentioned planning document by the end of the grant term.

• The activities described are eligible under the Guidelines, and the project's core infrastructure is clearly defined. We encourage you to submit a full application! For Early Infrastructure projects, a reminder that the Planning Document is the main deliverable and should meet the minimum requirements in Section 3.6 of the Guidelines, including priority areas for infrastructure, an infrastructure strategy, community-informed design principles, among other elements. For Advanced Infrastructure projects, the full application should demonstrate feasibility (capability of implementation within 48 months) by providing specific infrastructure and site, and other materials listed in Section 4.5 (preliminary cost estimates and preliminary design or engineering documents).

• The description does not address harm reduction. It doesn't establish the harm the community faces from extreme heat or connect those harms to the proposed infrastructure. Harm reduction is core to this program, so for the full application, the applicant will need to describe the current harm that specific groups face and how the infrastructure interrupts the pathway between extreme heat and those harms.

• The pre-application doesn't ask about Belonging directly, so we're reminding all applicants this is an area to develop for the full application. Belonging is about designing infrastructure shaped by the community and belonging to the community so that people actually use it and can receive the cooling benefits the infrastructure provides. We encourage the applicant to show how residents help shape the design, how the infrastructure reflects the community's identity, and how it will be welcoming to the community so that it is genuinely used. Belonging comes from the process of engaging people, not from any single feature. Please see Appendix B of the Guidelines. Full Applications are considered strong when the narrative clearly explains how the applicant plans to engage the community before, during, and after the project phase in order to ensure that any implemented infrastructure is a direct response to expressed community needs and desires, rather than something constructed without broad community input. Applicants should also consider the process by which they intend to create community ""buy-in"" and ownership towards the infrastructure that will last after the grant period, and how to detail these plans in the Full Application's narrative.

• Your application may not meet partnership requirements. If the Lead Applicant is a local or regional public entity, the Co-Applicant must be a CBO or CA Native American Tribe. If the Lead Applicant is a CBO, the Co-Applicant must be a local or regional public entity or CA Native American Tribe. If the Lead Applicant is an academic institution or a coalition, the Co-Applicants must be both a CBO and a local or regional public entity or CA Native American Tribe. Please see Section 6.3 of the Guidelines for Co-Applicant requirements. As the Lead Applicant is a Local Public Entity, the Co-Applicant must be either a CBO or a CA Native American Tribe.  Because of this partnership requirement, the CCRCD is not an eligible Co-Applicant.  Either the Bay Point Garden Club or the Caribbean South American Hispanic Council would be an eligible Co-Applicant. See Section 6.3 of the full program guidelines for partnership requirements, and Section 6.4 for other roles which the CCRCD could fill.

Applicant is encouraged to re-review the full program guidelines prior to the submission of a Full Application in order to ensure eligibility and compliance."
