**Project Description**
11\. Provide a concise description of the proposed project and scope of work. Include an explanation of how your project will mitigate the urban heat island effect, rising temperatures, and extreme heat impacts. Describe specific project deliverables and the estimated length of time it will take to complete the project (upload a conceptual site plan separately, if available).
**Ambrose Memorial Park is one of the primary recreational facility for the Bay Point community, owned and maintained by the Ambrose Recreation and Park District. It features a playground, picnic tables, BBQs, bathroom, large grass areas, and ‘Lion’s Den’ picnic area with picnic tables, large BBQ and electricity. It is also home of the Ambrose Aquatic Center. Bay Point is a disadvantaged community under SB 535, and the park's location adjacent to Highway 4 contributes to significant urban heat island conditions. The proposed urban greening project will implement nature-based solutions including expanded tree canopy, drought-tolerant native plantings, and enhanced shaded green spaces to provide critical cooling refuge, improved air quality, and equitable access to healthy outdoor recreation for one of Contra Costa County's most heat-vulnerable communities.**
**The need for this project is strongly supported by multiple data sources documenting Bay Point's extreme heat vulnerability:**
  - **The 2015 Contra Costa Health Services report Climate Change Vulnerability in Contra Costa County: A Focus on Heat identifies Bay Point as having high overall vulnerability to extreme heat, with elevated indicators for poverty, children under age 5, linguistic isolation, asthma, and treeless areas (pg 32)**
  - **Climate projections using Scripps Institute of Oceanography/Cal-Adapt data forecast 21–35 days over 100°F annually by the 2090s for the Bay Point area (pg 13)**
  - **The Climate Vulnerability Map designates the project site as having HIGH future vulnerability to extreme heat, with 15.6% of days projected to exceed 100°F by 2050, elevated PM 2.5 exposure, and urban heat island classification**
  - **The California Heat Assessment Tool rates the project area HIGH for Heat Health Events with a score of 3.55 (exceeding the 75th percentile) and MODERATE for the Heat Health Action Index with a score of 45.41**

13\. Describe any other benefits the proposed project is anticipated to provide that are not specifically included in question 12 above (e.g., increased recreational opportunities, improved health and/or safety, etc.)
**Beyond heat relief, the Ambrose Memorial Park Urban Greening Project will deliver a range of additional community and environmental benefits. A primary tributary of Willow Creek, intersects the park, and proposed green stormwater infrastructure and native riparian plantings will reduce flood risk and stormwater runoff during major rain events while improving flood management for the upper Willow Creek watershed and restoring critical native vegetation and habitat. The project is further strengthened by a recent land donation from Contra Costa County, which expands the project footprint. Vegetated barriers/Tree buffer will reduce PM 2.5 and air pollution exposure from highway 4 not only for park users but also for the daily commuters and travelers using the adjacent Bay Point/Pittsburg BART station and the Delta de Anza Regional Trail. This multi-benefit project therefore extends its positive impacts well beyond the park boundary, improving air quality, flood resilience, habitat, and green connectivity for a broad cross-section of the Bay Point community and the wider regional trail and transit network. In partnership with the Contra Costa Resource Conservation District (CCRCD), the Ecostewards program will lead implementation of the green infrastructure improvements, creating local jobs and workforce training opportunities for Bay Point residents while also providing environmental education programming for the community. The Ambrose Recreation and Park District will partner in the long-term maintenance of these improvements, ensuring the project's benefits are sustained for generations. Together, these elements represent a truly multi-benefit project that advances climate resilience, ecological restoration, community health, and economic opportunity in one of Contra Costa County's most vulnerable communities.**
14\. Explain the need for the proposed project. If applicable, explain how your project will provide “meaningful and direct” benefits to a disadvantaged community, a severely disadvantaged community, or a vulnerable population (as defined in Appendix D and described on pages 3-4 of the Grant Guidelines).
**Ambrose Memorial Park serves as one of the primary recreational green space for Bay Point, a disadvantaged community that faces some of the most severe and compounding heat vulnerabilities in Contra Costa County. The park's location adjacent to Highway 4 contributes to urban heat island conditions, elevated PM 2.5 exposure, and limited tree canopy, conditions that disproportionately burden a community already identified as having high indicators for poverty, linguistic isolation, asthma, and large populations of children under age 5. Without intervention, these conditions will worsen: climate projections forecast 21–35 days over 100°F annually in Bay Point by the 2090s, and the California Heat Assessment Tool rates the project area HIGH for Heat Health Events. The Ambrose Memorial Park Urban Greening Project will provide meaningful and direct benefits by creating tangible, community-identified cooling infrastructure, expanded tree canopy, drought-tolerant native plantings, and shaded gathering spaces, that directly reduce heat exposure for park users, improve air quality, and expand access to safe outdoor recreation for vulnerable residents including young children, low-income families, and linguistically isolated households. These benefits directly respond to community needs, build long-term climate resilience, and would not occur without grant funding, as the Ambrose Recreation and Park District serves a low-income community with limited resources to implement large-scale greening improvements independently.**
